When expertise is low, the board cannot verify metric construction; executives satisfy the link through reclassification, boundary changes, and target selection, so the incentive is absorbed by measurement and real reallocation stalls or reverses as capital is dressed as green—the trace is flat green-capex totals with frequent category redefinitions and methodological footnotes. The low-expertise logic is affirmative: weak verification actively creates the gaming opportunity. The claims are conditional and signed. Hypothesis 1: the effect of ESG-linked executive pay on green capital reallocation is more positive when board sustainability expertise is high. Hypothesis 2: the effect is weak or negative when expertise is low. No unconditional main-effect hypothesis is stated.
