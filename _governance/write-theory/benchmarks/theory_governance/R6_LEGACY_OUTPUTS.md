Rationale: We treat concession-making as a union-level decision taken during the window between automation announcement and implementation, with regional reemployment prospects as a categorical boundary state stated before its interpretation and mechanism. Rationale: We make no unconditional prediction, because the same managerial practice changes meaning with perceived evaluation stakes. Rationale: We model the shift as a stage transition driven by three actors: pilot members, holdout members, and the cooperative assembly. The transition condition is the assembly's judgment that per-member compliance cost has fallen below the premium buyers will pay, at which point a binding standard becomes proposable. Argument graph: `audit disclosure → {benefit: buyer uncertainty ↓ → retention ↑} vs {burden: violation publicity → reputational spillover → retention ↓}; dominance = (severity − expectation gap) × remediability`

Rationale: We derive two symmetric mechanisms bearing on the same evaluative outcome — the buyer's retention decision. The benefit mechanism: mandatory audit disclosure reduces the buyer's uncertainty about supplier conduct, converting a credence attribute into documented evidence, so buyers retain disclosed suppliers to protect continuity. The burden mechanism: the same disclosure publicizes violations and links them to the buyer's name, creating reputational spillover and remediation costs, so buyers terminate to sever the association. The reconciliation is theory-based rather than additive: which mechanism dominates depends on the gap between disclosed severity and the buyer's prior expectation, and on remediability. When violations are mild relative to expectations and correctable, the uncertainty-reduction benefit dominates because retention with remediation is cheaper than switching; when violations are severe or structurally unfixable, spillover risk dominates because continued association compounds exposure. Proposition 1: Mandatory human-rights audit disclosure increases buyers' supplier retention when disclosed violations fall below expectation and are remediable, and decreases retention when violations exceed expectation or resist remediation.

Argument graph: `playbook scripts → {structure benefit: cognitive off-loading → cue detection ↑ → adaptive selling ↑; early tenure} vs {script rigidity: narrowed cue attention → switching cost → adaptive selling ↓; late tenure}`

Rationale: Two countervailing mechanisms link standardized playbook scripts to adaptive selling, each with its own warrant. The structure-benefit mechanism: scripts codify diagnostic heuristics and off-load routine formulation, freeing working memory for cue detection; the warrant is cognitive-load reasoning about improvised conversation, so scripts should raise adaptation where sellers would otherwise improvise poorly. The script-rigidity mechanism: repeated script use narrows attention to script-anticipated cues and raises mid-conversation switching costs; the warrant is habituation and automaticity research, so scripts should depress responsiveness to unexpected customer signals. Observable traces diverge accordingly: benefit-dominant sellers ask more relevant probing questions and diagnose needs faster; rigidity-dominant sellers continue scripted lines after disqualifying cues and adjust plans less within calls. We state these as prose conditional predictions, not numbered hypotheses.

Rationale: Cross-sector convening capacity is an initiative-level capability to assemble actors from public, private, and civic domains and convert their participation into joint commitments, comprising three dimensions: convening reach (breadth of sectors mobilized), commitment conversion (turning attendance into binding pledges), and agenda stewardship (sustaining a shared decarbonization frame across meetings). Level: the regional initiative, not the individual convener; temporal scope: it accrues over repeated convening episodes and decays without them. Argument graph: `unlimited vacation → {autonomy restoration → discretionary detachment → recovery ↑ (H1a)} vs {norm-reference loss → self-exploitation → leave ↓ → recovery ↓ (H1b)}; same employee-year unit, 12-month horizon`

Rationale: Two theories yield opposed predictions for the same unit and horizon: the employee-year, over a twelve-month policy window. The autonomy-restoration account: recovery is constrained by control over work interruptions; unlimited vacation restores discretionary control, letting employees detach when strain accumulates, so recovery should rise — supported by job demands–resources reasoning that control buffers strain. The self-exploitation account: removing the explicit allowance deletes the social reference point for acceptable absence; employees compare against visible peer behavior and fear signaling low commitment, so they take less leave than under fixed entitlement and recovery falls — supported by social-norm and signaling reasoning. Given these competing arguments: H1a: Unlimited vacation policies increase employee recovery. H1b: Unlimited vacation policies decrease employee recovery.
