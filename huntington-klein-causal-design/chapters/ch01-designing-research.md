# Chapter 1: Designing Research

## Core Idea

A research design is the bridge from a question to quantitative empirical evidence capable of answering it. Data availability and statistical technique do not determine whether that bridge is valid.

## Design Contract

Specify five objects before analysis:

1. **Question**: What feature of the world is unknown?
2. **Claim type**: Descriptive, predictive, or causal?
3. **Evidence**: What observable pattern would bear on the question?
4. **Alternatives**: What other processes could generate that pattern?
5. **Design response**: What comparison or variation rules out those alternatives?

For causal work, add the intervention, outcome, unit, population, time horizon, and estimand.

## Workflow

1. Start with a well-defined question rather than a preferred dataset or estimator.
2. Translate the question into variables and a target comparison.
3. Describe how the observed data were generated, including selection into treatment and measurement.
4. Identify why a naive comparison may fail.
5. Search for variation that better approximates the target comparison.
6. Choose an estimator only after the design is explicit.

## Worked Example

> Source-grounded reconstruction from Huntington-Klein (2025); compressed and paraphrased.

The chapter uses highway lanes and congestion. A cross-sectional comparison can show more congestion on wider highways because congested routes are more likely to be widened. The observed association therefore mixes the effect of lanes with the process that assigned lanes. A useful design must isolate lane changes not generated by the same demand shocks that drive congestion.

## Design-Evidence Separation

| Layer | Question |
|---|---|
| Theory | Why should changing X alter Y? |
| Research question | Which causal contrast would inform that theory? |
| Design | Which observed comparison approximates the contrast? |
| Estimator | How is that comparison calculated? |
| Inference | How uncertain is the estimate under the design? |
| Claim | Which conclusion is licensed, for whom and when? |

A failure at an earlier layer cannot be repaired at a later layer. More precise inference cannot rescue the wrong comparison; a sophisticated estimator cannot make a vague treatment coherent.

## Failure Modes

- **Method-first design**: Picking regression, matching, or DiD before identifying the comparison.
- **Association substitution**: Answering “are X and Y related?” when the question is “what would changing X do to Y?”
- **Data determinism**: Redefining the question until it matches conveniently observed fields.
- **Unstated alternatives**: Treating the preferred causal story as the only DGP.

## Completion Check

The design is ready for Chapter 5 only when a skeptical reader can identify the intended comparison, its main rival explanations, and the proposed source of identifying variation.
