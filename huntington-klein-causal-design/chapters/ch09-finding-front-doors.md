# Chapter 9: Finding Front Doors

## Core Idea

When measuring and adjusting for every confounder is implausible, search for a source of treatment variation that is disconnected from the problematic back doors. Natural experiments, random assignment processes, instruments, cutoffs, and timing designs operationalize this strategy.

## Two Meanings to Keep Separate

- **Isolating causal/front-door variation**: the chapter's broad strategy of retaining treatment variation generated by a cleaner assignment process.
- **Pearl's front-door criterion**: a specific mediation-based identification result requiring strong conditions. It is specialized and should not be used as a synonym for any natural experiment.

## Variation-Isolation Workflow

1. List every reason treatment varies.
2. Identify the actor or rule producing each source.
3. Ask whether that source shares causes with the outcome.
4. Restrict, instrument, difference, or compare around a rule to isolate a cleaner source.
5. Define which units comply with or are local to that source.
6. Map the isolated variation to the resulting estimand.
7. search for sorting, anticipation, spillovers, concurrent shocks, and measurement responses.
8. design falsification tests tied to those threats.

## Natural-Experiment Standard

“As-if random” is a conclusion that needs an assignment-process argument. Document:

- the exact rule or event;
- who could influence assignment;
- when they learned about it;
- whether treatment affects inclusion or measurement;
- why nearby or comparison units face the same outcome-generating shocks;
- what discontinuities or imbalances would contradict the story.

## Worked Example

> Source-grounded reconstruction from Huntington-Klein (2025); compressed and paraphrased.

The chapter uses lottery winnings to show how restricting attention to players and using prize-size randomness can isolate a cleaner wealth shock. The lesson is not that all lotteries are valid; the researcher must verify prize assignment, sample conditioning, and the estimand among participants.

## Sources of Cleaner Variation

| Source | Identification idea | Threat to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit lottery/random rule | Assignment disconnects treatment from potential outcomes | Noncompliance, attrition, spillovers |
| Administrative cutoff | Local assignment jump | Sorting, other threshold changes |
| Judge/examiner assignment | Decision-maker tendency shifts treatment | Nonrandom routing, direct channels |
| Policy rollout | Timing creates treated/comparison changes | Differential trends, anticipation |
| External encouragement | Instrument-induced treatment | Exclusion and weak relevance |
| Within-unit shock | Compare unit to itself | Time-varying confounding |
| Formal front-door mediator | Mediation structure identifies effect | Mediator confounding and direct path |

The same label can hide different designs. A “policy shock” may support DiD, IV, event study, or none; route by the actual assignment process.

## Failure Modes

- Calling an external event exogenous because the researcher did not cause it.
- Using policy timing without modeling anticipation and coincident events.
- Assuming a narrow sample removes all selection rather than changes the target population.
- Applying the formal front-door method without verifying its mediator and confounding conditions.
