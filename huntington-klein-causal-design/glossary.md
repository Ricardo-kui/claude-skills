# Glossary

Terms are concise routing definitions. Open the cited chapter when a definition affects identification or implementation.

- **Adjustment set** — Variables conditioned on to block all relevant open back-door paths without opening colliders or blocking target causal paths. (Ch8)
- **Anticipation** — Behavioral or outcome response before recorded treatment because units expect treatment. (Ch17–18)
- **ATE** — Average treatment effect in the target population. (Ch10)
- **ATT** — Average treatment effect among treated units. (Ch10, Ch14, Ch18)
- **ATU/ATC** — Average treatment effect among untreated/control units. (Ch10)
- **Back-door path** — A treatment–outcome path entering treatment through an incoming arrow and capable of generating noncausal association. (Ch8)
- **Balance** — Similarity of weighted/matched treatment groups on observed pre-treatment covariate distributions. (Ch14)
- **Bandwidth** — Neighborhood around an RDD cutoff used for local estimation. (Ch20)
- **Bias** — Systematic difference between an estimator's expected value and its target parameter. (Ch13, Ch15)
- **Bootstrap** — Repeated resampling used to approximate estimator sampling behavior; resampling must preserve design dependence. (Ch15)
- **CATE** — Average treatment effect conditional on specified characteristics. (Ch10, Ch22)
- **Causal diagram / DAG** — Directed acyclic graph representing variables and causal relations in a DGP. (Ch6–7)
- **Causal discovery** — Algorithms that infer graph features from conditional-independence or score assumptions; generally hypothesis-generating. (Ch22)
- **Causal effect** — Contrast in an outcome under specified interventions/treatment states for a defined population and time. (Ch2, Ch6)
- **Clustered standard errors** — Inference allowing arbitrary error dependence within defined clusters. (Ch13, Ch16)
- **Collider** — A node where two arrows meet head-to-head on a path; conditioning can open that path. (Ch8)
- **Common support / overlap / positivity** — Availability of the treatment conditions needed for comparable units across the target covariate support. (Ch14)
- **Confounder** — A pre-treatment common cause on an open treatment–outcome back-door path. (Ch6–8)
- **Consistency** — Observed outcome under received treatment equals the corresponding potential outcome under the specified treatment version. (Ch23)
- **Counterfactual** — Outcome that would occur for the same causal unit under an alternative treatment state. (Ch2, Ch5)
- **DGP** — Data-generating process: the causal and measurement processes producing observed data. (Ch5)
- **Difference-in-differences** — Design comparing treated-group changes with credible untreated counterfactual changes. (Ch18)
- **Direct effect** — Effect transmitted without an intermediate causal node under a specified intervention. (Ch6)
- **Double machine learning** — Orthogonal-score and cross-fitting framework using flexible nuisance models under a separate identification design. (Ch22)
- **Entropy balancing** — Weighting method that directly enforces selected covariate moment balance. (Ch14)
- **Estimand** — Precisely defined population causal quantity targeted by a design. (Ch10)
- **Event study** — Design comparing post-event outcomes with a forecasted or comparison-based counterfactual. (Ch17)
- **Exclusion restriction** — Instrument affects outcome only through the modeled endogenous treatment pathway. (Ch19)
- **Fixed effects** — Estimation isolating variation within fixed-effect categories and absorbing category-constant factors. (Ch16)
- **Front-door criterion** — Specialized mediation-based identification result; distinct from broadly isolating clean treatment variation. (Ch9)
- **Fuzzy RDD** — Cutoff causes a discontinuous treatment-probability jump rather than deterministic assignment. (Ch20)
- **HAC** — Heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent inference for serial dependence. (Ch13, Ch17)
- **Heterogeneous treatment effects** — Effects that vary across units, contexts, cohorts, or time. (Ch10, Ch22)
- **Identification** — Conditions under which observed variation uniquely or set-identifies the target causal quantity. (Ch5)
- **Identified set** — Values of a target parameter consistent with data and maintained assumptions. (Ch21)
- **Imputation estimator** — Method predicting untreated potential outcomes from untreated observations and comparing them with treated outcomes. (Ch18)
- **Independence / exchangeability** — Treatment or instrument variation is unrelated to relevant potential outcomes, possibly conditional on covariates. (Ch14, Ch19)
- **Indirect effect** — Effect transmitted through one or more mediator nodes. (Ch6)
- **Inference** — Quantification of sampling/design uncertainty for an estimator; distinct from identification. (Ch13)
- **Instrument** — Variable used to isolate exogenous variation in an endogenous treatment. (Ch19)
- **Interference** — One unit's treatment affects another unit's outcome. (Ch23)
- **ITT** — Effect of treatment assignment or encouragement regardless of receipt. (Ch10, Ch19)
- **LATE** — Average effect for units whose treatment status is changed by the instrument under IV assumptions. (Ch10, Ch19)
- **Mediator** — Variable caused by treatment that transmits part of its effect to the outcome. (Ch6, Ch8)
- **Missingness mechanism** — Process determining which observations or values are unobserved. (Ch23)
- **Monotonicity** — IV condition excluding units whose treatment moves opposite to the instrument's intended direction. (Ch19)
- **Natural experiment** — External assignment process that creates plausibly as-if-random or otherwise clean variation. (Ch9)
- **Negative control** — Outcome, exposure, or period expected to show no effect under the design, used for falsification. (Ch11)
- **Parallel trends** — DiD assumption that treated and comparison groups would have followed the same untreated change path. (Ch18)
- **Partial identification** — Reporting bounds or an identified set after weakening assumptions required for a point estimate. (Ch21)
- **Placebo test** — Re-estimation using a fake treatment, date, cutoff, or outcome where no causal effect should exist. (Ch11)
- **Post-treatment variable** — Variable causally affected by treatment; generic adjustment can alter the estimand or induce bias. (Ch8)
- **Propensity score** — Conditional probability of treatment given pre-treatment covariates. (Ch14)
- **Regression discontinuity** — Local design exploiting a discontinuous assignment change at a running-variable cutoff. (Ch20)
- **Relevance** — IV condition that the instrument changes the endogenous treatment. (Ch19)
- **Residual** — Observed outcome minus model-predicted conditional outcome; not automatically unconfounded variation. (Ch4)
- **Robustness test** — Check that falsifies an assumption or estimates under a meaningfully different/relaxed assumption. (Ch11)
- **Sample weights** — Weights changing how sampled observations represent a target population. (Ch13)
- **Sharp RDD** — Treatment changes deterministically at the cutoff. (Ch20)
- **SUTVA** — Stable-unit-treatment-value condition covering well-defined treatment versions and no unmodeled interference. (Ch23)
- **Synthetic control** — Weighted donor combination used to construct an untreated counterfactual for one or a few treated units. (Ch22)
- **Treatment version** — Specific form, dose, implementation, or exposure hidden by a common treatment label. (Ch23)
- **TWFE** — Two-way fixed effects with unit/group and time effects; legacy pooled treatment coefficients can fail under staggered adoption and heterogeneous effects. (Ch16, Ch18)
- **Weak instrument** — Instrument whose limited first-stage information causes unstable, biased, or weakly identified IV inference. (Ch19)
- **Within variation** — Change inside a fixed-effect category after removing between-category differences. (Ch16)
