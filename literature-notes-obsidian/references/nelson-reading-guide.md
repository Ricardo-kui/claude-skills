# Nelson Reading Guide

Source: Michael J. Nelson, *How to Read Journal Articles Like a Professor*.

Use this reference for `writer` mode or when the user wants to understand how a paper is written rather than only what it says.

## Three Reading Purposes

### Reading for Substance

- What is the paper's main question?
- What answer does the paper offer?
- Where does the paper sit in the larger conversation?

### Reading as a Researcher

- What is the key comparison?
- What is the unit of analysis?
- How are the core variables measured?
- Does the paper have causal entitlement, and under what assumptions?
- Where is the design slippage between the ideal test and the actual test?
- Do the conclusions stay within the limits of the evidence?

### Reading as a Writer

- How are sections ordered and balanced?
- How does the introduction identify audience and establish importance?
- How are prior studies reviewed: one by one or thematically?
- How do theory, methods, and results sections manage pacing and signposting?
- Which information is placed in main text versus appendix?
- How are discussion and conclusion sections proportioned across summary, limitation, literature dialogue, and future research?

## Writer-Mode Extraction Prompts

- What does the first paragraph do?
- When does the paper move from phenomenon to literature?
- How does the paper show the gap rather than simply announce it?
- How are tables and figures narrated without becoming table-reading?
- Which paragraph or sentence moves are reusable rather than merely stylish?
