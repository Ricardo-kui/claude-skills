# Management and Organization Theory: A Jossey-Bass Reader

**Author**: Jeffrey A. Miles | **Edition**: 2012 | **Coverage**: introduction, 40 theories, conclusion | **Updated**: 2026-08-10

## Route the Request

Identify the branch before loading references.

- **Select or compare theories**: Read [cheatsheet.md](cheatsheet.md), then read the 2–4 closest candidate chapters. Apply the **anchor test** before answering.
- **Develop theory or hypotheses**: Read the relevant chapter(s) and the matching contract in [patterns.md](patterns.md). Apply the **anchor test**, then express the mechanism as a construct-level causal chain.
- **Diagnose an organizational problem**: Read [cheatsheet.md](cheatsheet.md) and 1–3 relevant chapters. Separate explanation, intervention, side effects, and limits.
- **Study a named theory**: Read its chapter directly. Read its `Connects To` chapters only when comparison or integration is requested.
- **Browse the collection**: Read [theory-index.md](theory-index.md). Use the topic index there to narrow the request before opening chapters.
- **Define a term**: Read [glossary.md](glossary.md). Open the cited chapter when the definition affects a substantive claim.
- **Make a publication-facing source claim**: Read the theory entry in [foundational-works.md](foundational-works.md), verify the original source when precision matters, and apply the **frontier check**.

## Common Intake

Before selecting a theory, identify the phenomenon, focal actor, level of analysis, context, and time. Infer missing details when the request supports a safe assumption; otherwise state the uncertainty that changes the theory choice.

Group studies by theoretical construct and mechanism. Treat measure, proxy, clock, and sample differences as operationalizations unless the theoretical object changes. Keep literature-established claims, the project's proposed connection, and untested judgments distinct.

Combine theories only when each supplies a distinct necessary mechanism or level transition. Express the integration as one causal architecture and identify where each theory enters it.

## Anchor Test

Complete theory selection only when the answer contains all six items:

1. One anchor theory.
2. At least one closest rival theory.
3. The mechanism that makes the anchor and rival non-interchangeable.
4. Construct levels, time order, and boundary conditions.
5. An observation or test that could distinguish or disconfirm the anchor explanation.
6. The epistemic split: established theory, project connection, and untested judgment.

For a simple named-theory explanation, items 1, 3, and 4 are sufficient unless the user requests comparison or research design.

## Frontier Check

Treat Miles (2012) as a structured entry point, not the current frontier. For publication-facing claims, verify original sources, construct validity, major reviews, and relevant post-2012 developments. Label book-derived research prompts as historical until that check is complete.

## Output Discipline

- Prefer mechanism fit over shared outcomes or vocabulary.
- Preserve level consistency; state aggregation, emergence, or cross-level influence explicitly.
- Keep descriptive, explanatory, predictive, and normative claims separate.
- Identify whether an application example is source-documented or constructed for reasoning.
- State when evidence is insufficient to choose among rival theories.
