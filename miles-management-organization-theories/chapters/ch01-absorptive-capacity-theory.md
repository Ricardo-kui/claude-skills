# Chapter 1: Absorptive Capacity Theory

## Core Idea

Organizations gain from external knowledge only when prior knowledge and internal routines let them recognize its value, assimilate it, transform it, and exploit it.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Absorptive capacity**: Use when explaining heterogeneous learning or innovation from similar external information.
  - Build a related knowledge base.
  - Search for and recognize valuable external knowledge.
  - Assimilate and translate it into the firm's language.
  - Transform or recombine it with existing knowledge.
  - Exploit it in products, routines, or decisions.
- **Potential versus realized absorptive capacity**: Acquisition and assimilation create potential; transformation and exploitation turn it into results.
- **Cumulative learning path**: Prior investment makes later absorption easier; neglect can create lockout and widening performance gaps.

## Key Concepts

- **Prior related knowledge**: The basis for recognizing and interpreting novelty.
- **Knowledge diversity**: Difference sufficient to enable novel recombination without becoming unintelligible.
- **Relative absorptive capacity**: Learning ability conditioned by similarity between source and recipient.
- **Knowledge sources**: Internal R&D, operations, interorganizational borrowing, and purchased expertise or technology.

## Mental Models

- Use **recognize → assimilate → transform → exploit** to locate where a knowledge-transfer project is failing.
- Think of R&D as both invention and a **learning infrastructure**.
- Expect proactive search from high-capacity firms and crisis-driven search from low-capacity firms.

## Anti-patterns

- **Buying knowledge without receiving capacity**: Technology or consultants cannot substitute for internal understanding.
- **Treating exposure as learning**: Access to information does not demonstrate assimilation or use.
- **Maximizing similarity**: Knowledge too close adds little; knowledge too distant cannot be absorbed.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

Two manufacturers license the same AI quality-control system. Firm A has process engineers, cross-functional translation routines, and pilot-review teams; it adapts the model and reduces defects. Firm B delegates installation to the vendor and retains no internal learning routine; use collapses after the contract ends. The difference is realized absorptive capacity, not access.

## Key Takeaways

1. Measure acquisition, assimilation, transformation, and exploitation separately where possible.
2. Treat knowledge accumulation as path dependent.
3. Test environmental turbulence, source distance, and firm size as plausible boundaries.
4. Distinguish having external ties from learning through them.
