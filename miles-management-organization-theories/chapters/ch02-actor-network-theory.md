# Chapter 2: Actor-Network Theory

## Core Idea

Outcomes emerge from shifting relations among human and nonhuman actants; technologies, documents, standards, and people acquire agency-like effects through the network in which they are assembled.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Sociology of translation**: Use when an innovation or policy succeeds only through alignment of heterogeneous actors.
  - **Problematization**: Define the problem so the focal actor becomes indispensable.
  - **Interessement**: Stabilize proposed identities and interests.
  - **Enrollment**: Negotiate and coordinate roles.
  - **Mobilization**: Ensure spokespersons actually represent those claimed.
- **Relational materiality**: Explain an entity by the relations that give it effects, rather than by fixed intrinsic properties.
- **Generalized symmetry**: Trace humans and nonhumans with the same empirical discipline instead of presuming only people matter.

## Key Concepts

- **Actant**: Any human or nonhuman entity that makes a difference in a course of action.
- **Network**: A local chain of transformations and relations, not simply a social graph.
- **Translation**: Reframing interests and actions so heterogeneous entities become aligned.
- **Black box**: A stabilized arrangement whose internal negotiations are no longer questioned.

## Mental Models

- Use **follow the actors** when categories obscure how a practice is assembled.
- Treat technology as a participant that enables, constrains, records, and redirects action.
- Ask where translation fails rather than assuming resistance is a stable trait.

## Anti-patterns

- **Human-only explanations**: Ignoring forms, algorithms, equipment, standards, or physical layouts.
- **Fixed categories**: Starting with user/nonuser or organization/environment rather than observing how boundaries are produced.
- **Calling every connection a network**: ANT requires tracing transformations and associations.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A hospital's new medication system is not adopted by persuading clinicians alone. Barcode scanners, drug labels, database fields, carts, shift routines, professional authority, and exception alerts must all be enrolled. A poorly printed label can redirect the entire network. Analyze adoption by tracing these translations, not by reducing failure to employee attitude.

## Key Takeaways

1. Define actors empirically by the differences they produce.
2. Trace enrollment, breakdown, and repair over time.
3. Avoid imposing macro explanations before reconstructing local associations.
4. Use ANT for process explanation; combine it with other theories when prediction or evaluative criteria are needed.
