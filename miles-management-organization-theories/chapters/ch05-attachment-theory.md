# Chapter 5: Attachment Theory

## Core Idea

Early and ongoing relationship experiences form internal working models of self and others that shape proximity seeking, trust, support, and responses to threat in later relationships, including work relationships.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Attachment system**: Use when behavior changes under relational threat, separation, uncertainty, or need for a secure base.
- **Internal working models**: Expectations about one's own worthiness and others' availability guide interpretation and interaction.
- **Attachment orientations**:
  - **Secure**: Comfortable with closeness and autonomy.
  - **Anxious**: Heightened proximity seeking and fear of rejection.
  - **Avoidant**: Deactivation of dependency and discomfort with closeness.
- **Secure base / safe haven**: Relationships support exploration when available in ordinary work and protection when threat rises.

## Key Concepts

- **Proximity seeking**: Attempts to maintain access to a trusted figure.
- **Attachment anxiety**: Concern that support will be unavailable or withdrawn.
- **Attachment avoidance**: Preference for self-reliance and emotional distance.
- **Caregiving system**: The complementary capacity to notice and respond to another's need.

## Mental Models

- Use attachment theory for **relational regulation under threat**, not as a universal personality label.
- Treat orientation as a tendency that can vary across relationships and contexts.
- Match supervisory support to employees' needs without pathologizing difference.

## Anti-patterns

- **Diagnosing employees**: Attachment measures are not casual clinical labels.
- **One leadership relationship fits all**: Identical closeness and feedback can affect employees differently.
- **Ignoring context**: Toxic or unreliable environments can activate insecurity even among generally secure people.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

During restructuring, one employee repeatedly seeks reassurance, another withdraws, and a third asks for concrete information and continues working. A manager provides reliable schedules, individualized check-ins, clear boundaries, and consistent follow-through. The goal is not to classify people permanently but to create a dependable secure base during threat.

## Key Takeaways

1. Study attachment when security, trust, support, or separation is theoretically central.
2. Distinguish anxiety from avoidance.
3. Measure relationship-specific and contextual activation when relevant.
4. Avoid translating attachment orientation directly into competence or commitment.
