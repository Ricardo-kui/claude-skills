# Chapter 7: Balance Theory

## Core Idea

People prefer consistency among sentiments linking themselves, other people, and objects; imbalanced relational triads create pressure to change an attitude, relationship, or categorization.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **P–O–X triad**: Model a focal person (P), another actor (O), and an object or issue (X), with positive or negative relations among each pair.
- **Balance rule**: A triad is balanced when the product of relation signs is positive; imbalance motivates cognitive or relational change.
- **Consistency restoration**: Restore balance by changing sentiment, changing a relationship, differentiating the object, or leaving the situation.

## Key Concepts

- **Sentiment**: Positive or negative evaluation linking an actor to another actor or object.
- **Balanced state**: A configuration experienced as coherent and low in change pressure.
- **Imbalanced state**: An inconsistent configuration that produces tension.
- **Unit relation**: Perceived belonging or association that makes entities part of one cognitive unit.

## Mental Models

- Use triads when attitudes depend on **who else supports or rejects an object**.
- Look for balance-restoring moves during alliances, endorsements, brand crises, and change programs.
- Treat balance pressure as a tendency; salience and relationship strength affect whether action follows.

## Anti-patterns

- **Reducing all inconsistency to balance theory**: The distinctive unit is a relational triad.
- **Assuming rational evaluation**: Sentiment and association may dominate instrumental evidence.
- **Ignoring hierarchy**: Strong identities or valued relationships can outweigh weak object attitudes.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

An employee likes a trusted colleague but dislikes a new analytics system the colleague champions. The triad is imbalanced. Balance may be restored by warming to the system, distancing from the colleague, or redefining the system as useful only for a narrow task. A change leader can use credible peer endorsement and trial experience to shift the weakest link.

## Key Takeaways

1. Specify all three entities and relation signs.
2. Identify which relation is easiest to change.
3. Use longitudinal data to observe restoration rather than infer it from a snapshot.
4. Consider strength, salience, and alternative triads as boundaries.
