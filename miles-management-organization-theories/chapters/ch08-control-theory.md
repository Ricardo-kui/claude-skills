# Chapter 8: Control Theory

## Core Idea

Self-regulation operates through feedback loops that compare a sensed state with a reference standard and activate corrective behavior when a discrepancy is detected.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Cybernetic feedback loop**:
  - **Reference standard** defines the desired state.
  - **Sensor** observes the current state.
  - **Comparator** evaluates discrepancy.
  - **Effector** changes behavior or conditions.
- **Hierarchical control**: Lower-level task standards serve higher-level goals; conflict or overload arises when multiple loops compete.
- **Input, behavior, and output control**: Choose what to regulate based on measurability, task knowledge, and uncertainty.
- **Discrepancy reduction**: People may increase effort, change strategy, revise the standard, disengage, or reframe the comparison.

## Key Concepts

- **Negative feedback**: Action reduces deviation from a standard.
- **Positive feedback**: Action amplifies deviation; it is less common as a stable human-control mechanism.
- **Rate of progress**: Affect can reflect whether discrepancy is closing faster or slower than expected.
- **Goal hierarchy**: Nested standards connecting concrete acts to abstract purposes.

## Mental Models

- Use **standard → sense → compare → act → resense** to design performance systems.
- Diagnose whether failure lies in an unclear standard, weak sensing, faulty comparison, or ineffective action.
- Expect people to abandon or revise standards when discrepancies appear unattainable.

## Anti-patterns

- **Feedback without a controllable response**: Discrepancy information alone can create helplessness.
- **Metric substitution**: Sensors become targets and displace the underlying goal.
- **Single-loop management**: Employees juggle multiple standards and limited attention.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A call center sets a quality standard, samples calls, compares performance against the standard, and coaches agents on specific gaps. When average handling time is added without hierarchy, agents rush calls and quality falls. The remedy is to clarify priority, monitor both loops, and give effectors—training, scripts, authority—that can actually close gaps.

## Key Takeaways

1. Make standards specific, observable, and jointly understood.
2. Provide timely sensing and actionable feedback.
3. Study how people choose among competing loops.
4. Distinguish discrepancy detection from the strategy used to resolve it.
