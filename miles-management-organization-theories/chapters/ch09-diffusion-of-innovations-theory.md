# Chapter 9: Diffusion of Innovations Theory

## Core Idea

Innovations spread through communication over time within a social system; adoption depends on perceived attributes, decision stages, network influence, and implementation experience.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Perceived innovation attributes**: Adoption accelerates with relative advantage, compatibility, low complexity, trialability, and observability.
- **Innovation-decision process**: Knowledge → persuasion → decision → implementation → confirmation.
- **Adopter categories**: Innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, and laggards differ in timing and social role, not inherent worth.
- **S-curve**: Cumulative adoption starts slowly, accelerates after social proof and infrastructure accumulate, then saturates.

## Key Concepts

- **Innovation**: An idea, practice, service, or object perceived as new by the adopting unit.
- **Communication channel**: Means through which participants create and exchange information.
- **Opinion leader**: A socially credible actor who shapes others' interpretations.
- **Reinvention**: Modification of an innovation during adoption and use.

## Mental Models

- Use **attribute diagnosis** when an objectively useful innovation is spreading slowly.
- Separate awareness, favorable attitude, initial decision, competent implementation, and continued use.
- Treat diffusion as social learning, not information broadcast.

## Anti-patterns

- **Equating installation with adoption**.
- **Labeling late adopters irrational** without assessing compatibility, risk, or resources.
- **Assuming one-way communication** from change agent to passive audience.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A firm introduces a forecasting tool. Demonstrations raise observability, a sandbox raises trialability, integration with existing workflows raises compatibility, and peer champions translate its advantage. Usage initially grows slowly, then accelerates once early adopters provide credible local evidence. Track each decision stage to distinguish rejection from failed implementation.

## Key Takeaways

1. Define the adopting unit and what is perceived as new.
2. Measure adoption timing and implementation quality separately.
3. Model networks, channels, and opinion leadership.
4. Expect reinvention and discontinuance rather than uniform use.
