# Chapter 10: Dynamic Capabilities Theory

## Core Idea

Firms adapt to or shape change by purposefully sensing opportunities and threats, seizing them, and transforming their resource base and operating routines.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Zero-order versus dynamic capabilities**: Ordinary capabilities execute the current business; dynamic capabilities change what the organization can do.
- **Sense–seize–transform**:
  - Sense shifts through search, learning, and interpretation.
  - Seize by committing to business models, investments, and strategic choices.
  - Transform by recombining assets, structures, and routines.
- **Capability lifecycle**: Capabilities develop, branch, replicate, renew, or decline through use and path-dependent experience.

## Key Concepts

- **Resource base**: Physical, human, organizational, and relational assets available for reconfiguration.
- **Microfoundations**: Individual, process, and structural mechanisms underlying higher-level capability.
- **Path dependence**: Current options reflect accumulated history and prior commitments.
- **Environmental dynamism**: Rate and unpredictability of relevant change.

## Mental Models

- Use dynamic capabilities to explain **systematic strategic change**, not any successful response.
- Ask whether the organization can repeat the change process, not merely whether one transformation succeeded.
- Distinguish capability presence from capability quality and outcomes.

## Anti-patterns

- **Tautology**: Inferring superior capability from superior performance.
- **Renaming ad hoc problem solving** as a dynamic capability.
- **Ignoring costs**: Continuous reconfiguration can destroy reliable operating capabilities.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A manufacturer senses demand for low-carbon products, seizes it by committing capital and a new service model, and transforms procurement, engineering, incentives, and partnerships. A one-off executive intervention would be ad hoc; a repeatable process of scanning, investment governance, and resource recombination constitutes a stronger capability claim.

## Key Takeaways

1. Specify the capability and the resource base it changes.
2. Observe routines or microfoundations independently of performance.
3. State the environmental conditions requiring reconfiguration.
4. Compare benefits with disruption and maintenance costs.
