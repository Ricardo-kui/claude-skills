# Chapter 12: Ethical Theory

## Core Idea

Ethical theories provide rival criteria for judging what actors ought to do; responsible decisions require making the criterion explicit and confronting conflicts among consequences, duties, rights, justice, and character.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Virtue ethics**: Ask what a practically wise and good actor would become or do.
- **Consequentialism / teleology**: Judge actions by the value of their consequences and their distribution.
- **Deontology**: Judge actions by duties, rights, rules, or principles that constrain permissible conduct.
- **Justice approaches**: Examine fairness of procedures, distributions, recognition, and burdens.
- **Ethical pluralism**: Use multiple lenses to expose trade-offs rather than silently switching criteria.

## Key Concepts

- **Moral agent**: Actor capable of responsibility and ethical judgment.
- **Stakeholder impact**: Benefits, harms, rights, and exposure distributed across affected parties.
- **Universalizability**: Whether a rule could coherently guide all actors in comparable circumstances.
- **Moral intensity**: Features that make an ethical issue more salient and urgent.

## Mental Models

- Use a **multi-lens audit**: consequences, duties, rights, justice, and virtues.
- Separate descriptive social consensus from normative justification.
- Make uncertainty and irreversibility part of the ethical analysis.

## Anti-patterns

- **Ethics equals compliance**: Legal permission is not moral sufficiency.
- **Single-metric utilitarianism**: Aggregate benefit can conceal concentrated harm.
- **Values language without decision rules**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A platform can deploy a monitoring tool that raises productivity but intrudes on privacy. Consequentialism compares benefits and harms; deontology asks whether surveillance violates duties or rights; justice asks who bears risk and who gains; virtue ethics asks what kind of employer the practice creates. The decision record should state how conflicts were resolved and what safeguards follow.

## Key Takeaways

1. State the ethical criterion before evaluating conduct.
2. Identify affected stakeholders and asymmetries of power.
3. Test the decision under competing lenses.
4. Distinguish ethical theory from observed moral behavior.
