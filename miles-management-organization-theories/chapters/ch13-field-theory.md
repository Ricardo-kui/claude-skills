# Chapter 13: Field Theory

## Core Idea

Behavior at a given moment is a function of the person and the psychologically experienced environment—the total field of interdependent forces in the current life space.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **B = f(P, E)**: Explain behavior through the joint configuration of person and environment rather than either alone.
- **Life space**: Map all currently relevant perceptions, goals, barriers, people, and regions affecting action.
- **Force-field analysis**: Identify driving and restraining forces sustaining a quasi-stationary equilibrium.
- **Unfreezing–moving–refreezing**: Change the force configuration, enact movement, and stabilize a new pattern; treat this as a heuristic rather than a rigid three-step recipe.

## Key Concepts

- **Psychological field**: The situation as experienced by the actor at that time.
- **Valence**: Attractiveness or aversiveness of a region or goal.
- **Tension**: Motivational pressure arising from needs or force imbalance.
- **Boundary**: Perceived separation that enables or restricts movement.

## Mental Models

- Use **current total situation** when isolated traits or incentives fail to explain behavior.
- Change can come from strengthening drivers or weakening restraints; reducing restraints often creates less resistance.
- Map the employee's perceived field, not only management's objective environment.

## Anti-patterns

- **Listing forces without interdependence**.
- **Treating the field as static background**.
- **Assuming manager and employee inhabit the same life space**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

An employee delays a client report. Drivers include promotion goals and professional pride; restraints include ambiguous data, fear of blame, competing deadlines, and a colleague normalizing delay. Instead of adding pressure, the manager clarifies ownership, removes a data barrier, and creates safe review. The equilibrium shifts because restraints weaken.

## Key Takeaways

1. Define the focal actor, behavior, moment, and perceived field.
2. Map driving and restraining forces together.
3. Study change as reconfiguration of a system.
4. Reassess the field after intervention because forces adapt.
