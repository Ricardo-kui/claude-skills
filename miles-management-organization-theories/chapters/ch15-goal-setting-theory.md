# Chapter 15: Goal Setting Theory

## Core Idea

Specific, challenging goals raise performance by directing attention, mobilizing effort, sustaining persistence, and prompting strategy development—when ability, commitment, feedback, and resources are adequate.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Goal mechanisms**: Direction, effort, persistence, and task strategy.
- **Goal-performance moderators**: Commitment, task knowledge, self-efficacy, feedback, task complexity, and situational support.
- **Learning versus performance goals**: Use learning goals for novel, complex tasks; use performance goals when effective strategies are known.
- **Goal hierarchy**: Align proximal and distal goals while guarding against conflict and narrow focus.

## Key Concepts

- **Goal difficulty**: Level of challenge relative to capability.
- **Goal specificity**: Precision of the desired result.
- **Goal commitment**: Determination to attain the goal.
- **Feedback**: Information that permits progress assessment and strategy adjustment.

## Mental Models

- Use **specific + difficult + accepted + supported + fed back**.
- Goals focus attention; what is omitted can be neglected.
- When strategy is unknown, prescribe learning and experimentation before a hard output target.

## Anti-patterns

- **Stretch goals without capability or resources**.
- **Too many goals**: Attention fragments or actors choose the easiest metric.
- **Metric gaming**: Goal attainment displaces the underlying purpose.
- **Assuming participation always improves performance**: Its value often operates through information and commitment.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A product team receives a launch-date target but lacks knowledge about a new technology. A pure performance goal encourages shortcuts. Management first sets learning goals—complete experiments and document failure modes—then sets specific quality and schedule goals once strategy is clearer, with feedback and priority rules for trade-offs.

## Key Takeaways

1. Match goal type to task novelty and complexity.
2. Provide feedback and credible means of attainment.
3. Audit omitted dimensions and unethical shortcuts.
4. Measure commitment and self-efficacy as mechanisms or boundaries.
