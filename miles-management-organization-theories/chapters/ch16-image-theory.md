# Chapter 16: Image Theory

## Core Idea

Decision makers screen options for compatibility with principles, desired futures, and plans; they often reject incompatible options quickly and compare surviving options only afterward.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Three images**:
  - **Value image**: Principles, morals, identity, and standards.
  - **Trajectory image**: Desired future states and goals.
  - **Strategic image**: Plans and tactics for reaching them.
- **Compatibility test**: Count violations between an option and relevant images; reject options exceeding a tolerance threshold.
- **Adoption versus progress decisions**: Adoption screens new goals or plans; progress decisions assess whether current strategy is moving toward the trajectory.
- **Framing**: The subset of images considered relevant defines the decision context.

## Key Concepts

- **Image**: A schema representing principles, ends, or means.
- **Violation threshold**: Maximum incompatibility tolerated before rejection.
- **Screening**: Noncompensatory elimination before detailed choice.
- **Progress decision**: Evaluation of whether the status quo remains acceptable.

## Mental Models

- Use **screen first, choose second** to explain rejection of objectively attractive options.
- Diagnose resistance by locating conflict with values, trajectory, or strategy.
- Expect action when current progress falls beyond an acceptable tolerance.

## Anti-patterns

- **Assuming exhaustive utility calculation**.
- **Treating stated values as the only active image**.
- **Adding benefits to compensate for a nonnegotiable principle violation**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

An employee rejects a lucrative transfer. Compensation fits, but the role conflicts with a value image of family responsibility and a trajectory image of becoming a technical expert. More pay cannot compensate for a threshold violation. A redesigned local expert path may pass the screen and merit comparison.

## Key Takeaways

1. Identify value, trajectory, and strategic images separately.
2. Determine which violations are noncompensatory.
3. Distinguish adoption from progress decisions.
4. Use image theory for descriptive decision processes, not normative optimization.
