# Chapter 17: Institutional Theory

## Core Idea

Organizations adopt and retain structures not only for technical efficiency but also because regulative, normative, and cultural-cognitive institutions make practices legitimate, expected, and taken for granted.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Three institutional pillars**: Regulative rules and sanctions, normative obligations, and cultural-cognitive schemas.
- **Isomorphic pressures**: Coercive dependence, mimetic imitation under uncertainty, and normative professionalization.
- **Legitimacy mechanism**: Conformity can secure resources, acceptance, and survival even when technical benefits are unclear.
- **Strategic responses**: Acquiescence → compromise → avoidance → defiance → manipulation, increasing in active resistance.
- **Institutional entrepreneurship**: Actors mobilize resources to create or transform field-level institutions.

## Key Concepts

- **Organizational field**: Interacting organizations and actors constituting a recognized institutional domain.
- **Institutionalization**: Process through which a practice becomes stable and taken for granted.
- **Decoupling**: Separation between formal structures and operational practice.
- **Institutional work**: Purposive action that creates, maintains, or disrupts institutions.

## Mental Models

- Use **efficiency versus legitimacy** as competing or joint explanations.
- Identify which pressure operates, from whom, and at what level.
- Treat organizations as capable of compliance, translation, resistance, and change.

## Anti-patterns

- **Calling any similarity isomorphism** without a pressure mechanism.
- **Treating organizations as passive prisoners**.
- **Labeling incremental internal change institutional entrepreneurship** without field-level consequence.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

Hospitals adopt a fashionable quality certification. Regulators make it coercive, professional associations make it normative, and uncertain hospitals copy prestigious peers. Some integrate routines; others decouple ceremonial documentation from practice. The same adoption indicator therefore masks different mechanisms and implementation.

## Key Takeaways

1. Distinguish regulative, normative, and cognitive mechanisms.
2. Measure legitimacy and conformity separately from efficiency.
3. Specify field, audience, status, and time.
4. Examine variation, resistance, and decoupling after adoption.
