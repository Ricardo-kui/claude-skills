# Chapter 18: Knowledge-Based Theory

## Core Idea

Firms exist and differ because they coordinate, integrate, store, and apply specialized knowledge more effectively than alternative arrangements.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Knowledge types**: Know-what, know-how, know-why, know-when, and know-who.
- **Tacit–explicit distinction**: Tacit knowledge is embodied and learned through practice; explicit knowledge can be articulated, codified, and transferred more readily.
- **Knowledge integration**: Organizations coordinate specialists through rules, sequencing, routines, group problem solving, and shared language.
- **Articulation and codification**: Make knowledge expressible, record it, standardize it, and disseminate it—where the knowledge permits.

## Key Concepts

- **Tacit knowledge**: Difficult-to-articulate knowing embedded in action and experience.
- **Explicit knowledge**: Knowledge represented in communicable form.
- **Common knowledge**: Shared language and understanding enabling integration.
- **Combinative capability**: Capacity to synthesize and apply distributed knowledge.

## Mental Models

- Use **where does knowledge reside, and how is it integrated?**
- Choose transfer mechanisms based on tacitness, complexity, and dependence.
- Preserve valuable tacit skill through apprenticeship, communities, and repeated joint practice—not documentation alone.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating information repositories as organizational knowledge**.
- **Codify everything**: Some knowledge loses meaning outside practice.
- **Ignoring incentives and power in knowledge sharing**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A retiring engineer can diagnose machine noise that no manual captures. Interviews yield partial rules, but shadowing, annotated cases, paired troubleshooting, and repeated feedback transfer more of the skill. The firm combines codification with socialization instead of assuming a database can replace embodied judgment.

## Key Takeaways

1. Specify knowledge content, holder, recipient, and integration task.
2. Distinguish creation, transfer, storage, retrieval, and application.
3. Match coordination to tacitness and specialization.
4. Do not infer knowledge from patents or documents alone.
