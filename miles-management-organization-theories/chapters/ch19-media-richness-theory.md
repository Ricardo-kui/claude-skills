# Chapter 19: Media Richness Theory

## Core Idea

Communication effectiveness depends on fit between a task's equivocality and a medium's capacity for rapid feedback, multiple cues, language variety, and personal focus.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Richness hierarchy**: Face-to-face interaction is conventionally richer than interactive audio, personalized text, and impersonal numeric documents.
- **Equivocality–richness fit**: Use richer media when multiple plausible interpretations require negotiation; lean media can efficiently transmit clear information.
- **Uncertainty versus equivocality**: Uncertainty is missing information; equivocality is ambiguity about meaning.
- **Symbolic and social influence**: Media choice also conveys urgency, formality, status, and relational intent.

## Key Concepts

- **Immediate feedback**: Speed of clarification and adjustment.
- **Multiple cues**: Verbal, visual, vocal, and contextual signals.
- **Personal focus**: Ability to tailor communication to a recipient.
- **Media repertoire**: Set of available channels used in combination.

## Mental Models

- Use **task ambiguity × medium capacity** rather than assuming rich is always better.
- Separate initial sensemaking from later documentation; different phases may need different media.
- Account for user experience and socially constructed norms around a medium.

## Anti-patterns

- **One fixed ranking for all technologies and users**.
- **Using richness to explain access or adoption** without a fit mechanism.
- **Delivering sensitive, equivocal messages through lean channels only**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A reorganization is announced in a detailed email. Information is abundant, but employees interpret implications differently. Leaders hold interactive meetings for meaning negotiation, follow with written role details, and use a searchable FAQ. The effective repertoire combines rich sensemaking with lean recordkeeping.

## Key Takeaways

1. Diagnose uncertainty and equivocality separately.
2. Define richness through actual affordances in context.
3. Match channel combinations to communication phases.
4. Measure communication performance, not media preference alone.
