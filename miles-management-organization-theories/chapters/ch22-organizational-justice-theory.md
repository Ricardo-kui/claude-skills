# Chapter 22: Organizational Justice Theory

## Core Idea

Employees judge fairness through outcomes, procedures, interpersonal treatment, and explanations; these judgments shape trust, acceptance, emotion, and behavior.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Distributive justice**: Evaluate outcomes using equity, equality, need, or another allocation rule.
- **Procedural justice**: Evaluate consistency, bias suppression, accuracy, correctability, representativeness, and ethicality of decision procedures.
- **Interpersonal justice**: Assess dignity, respect, and propriety of treatment.
- **Informational justice**: Assess truthfulness, adequacy, specificity, and timeliness of explanations.
- **Fair-process effect**: Fair procedures can increase acceptance of unfavorable outcomes without making every outcome fair.

## Key Concepts

- **Voice**: Opportunity to express relevant information or views.
- **Equity**: Outcomes proportional to contributions.
- **Correctability**: Opportunity to revise erroneous decisions.
- **Justice climate**: Shared group perception of how fairly the organization acts.

## Mental Models

- Use **outcome–process–treatment–explanation** as a four-part audit.
- Match the allocation rule to the legitimate purpose rather than treating equity as universal.
- Fairness requires both conduct and credible perception; transparency helps but cannot rescue an unjust rule.

## Anti-patterns

- **Outcome-only fairness**.
- **Voice theater**: Inviting input after the decision is fixed.
- **Using explanations to rationalize biased procedures**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

After budget cuts, two employees lose bonuses. The outcome is unfavorable, but management uses consistent criteria, checks data, allows appeal, communicates early, and treats employees respectfully. Acceptance may improve. If the criteria systematically disadvantage one group, however, polite explanation does not cure injustice.

## Key Takeaways

1. Keep justice dimensions conceptually distinct.
2. Specify the allocation rule and comparison referent.
3. Examine source, target, event, and social context.
4. Measure behavioral response without equating satisfaction with fairness.
