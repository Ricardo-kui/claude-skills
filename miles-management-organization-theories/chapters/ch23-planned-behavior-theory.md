# Chapter 23: Planned Behavior Theory

## Core Idea

Behavioral intention is shaped by attitude, subjective norm, and perceived behavioral control; intention predicts action when actors possess sufficient actual control.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Attitude pathway**: Behavioral beliefs × evaluation of consequences produce attitude toward the specific behavior.
- **Norm pathway**: Normative beliefs × motivation to comply produce subjective norm.
- **Control pathway**: Control beliefs × perceived power produce perceived behavioral control.
- **Intention–behavior link**: Intention combines the three pathways; perceived and actual control determine whether intention becomes action.

## Key Concepts

- **Behavioral intention**: Readiness to perform a defined behavior.
- **Subjective norm**: Perceived social pressure from relevant referents.
- **Perceived behavioral control**: Belief about ease, capability, opportunity, and obstacles.
- **Compatibility principle**: Measures should match behavior in action, target, context, and time.

## Mental Models

- Use **want to, expected to, able to**.
- Diagnose the weakest belief pathway before designing intervention.
- Separate perceived control from objective resources and constraints.

## Anti-patterns

- **Predicting broad behavior from broad attitudes**.
- **Treating intention as behavior**.
- **Using perceived control as a synonym for self-efficacy without considering external control**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

Employees intend to report safety near-misses only when they believe reporting helps, important peers approve, and the process is feasible without retaliation or excessive effort. Training changes attitude but will fail if supervisors punish reporters or the system is inaccessible. Measure each pathway and actual reports.

## Key Takeaways

1. Define behavior precisely by action, target, context, and time.
2. Elicit salient beliefs before selecting measures.
3. Measure intention close enough to action while allowing temporal ordering.
4. Include actual-control barriers when interpreting intention gaps.
