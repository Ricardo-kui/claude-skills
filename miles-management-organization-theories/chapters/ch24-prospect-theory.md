# Chapter 24: Prospect Theory

## Core Idea

Under risk, people evaluate gains and losses relative to a reference point, weigh losses more heavily than equivalent gains, and transform probabilities nonlinearly.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Reference dependence**: Outcomes are coded as changes from a salient status quo, aspiration, expectation, or comparison.
- **Value function**: Concave over gains, convex over losses, and steeper for losses; predicts risk aversion in gains and risk seeking in losses in standard cases.
- **Probability weighting**: Small probabilities may be overweighted and moderate-to-large probabilities underweighted.
- **Editing and evaluation**: Decision makers frame, simplify, and code prospects before valuing them.

## Key Concepts

- **Loss aversion**: A loss usually has greater psychological impact than an equal gain.
- **Certainty effect**: Disproportionate weight placed on certain outcomes.
- **Framing effect**: Equivalent options evoke different choices when presented as gains or losses.
- **Narrow bracketing**: Evaluating a decision separately rather than within the total portfolio.

## Mental Models

- Use **relative to what reference point?** before predicting risk preference.
- Expect threatened actors below aspiration levels to accept risks they would reject in the gain domain.
- Distinguish descriptive choice behavior from normative advice.

## Anti-patterns

- **Calling any risky behavior prospect theory** without reference point and value mechanism.
- **Assuming a fixed reference point**.
- **Equating loss aversion with all negativity effects**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

Two divisions face the same forecast. One is told it can preserve 90% of jobs; the other that 10% will be lost. The first framing places attention in gains and favors certainty; the second emphasizes loss and may increase support for a risky rescue. Researchers must identify the operative reference point rather than the wording alone.

## Key Takeaways

1. Specify outcome domain and reference point.
2. Model value and probability weighting separately.
3. Track how organizational aspirations and prior outcomes shift frames.
4. Compare with expected utility and threat-rigidity explanations.
