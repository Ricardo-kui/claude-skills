# Chapter 26: Resource-Based Theory

## Core Idea

Persistent performance differences can arise from valuable resource bundles that are heterogeneously distributed, imperfectly mobile, and difficult for rivals to imitate or substitute.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **VRIN/VRIO audit**: Ask whether a resource is valuable, rare, costly to imitate, nonsubstitutable, and whether the organization can capture its value.
- **Isolating mechanisms**: Historical conditions, causal ambiguity, social complexity, property rights, and time-compression diseconomies protect advantage.
- **Resource–capability distinction**: Resources are assets; capabilities are patterned capacities to deploy and combine assets.
- **Competitive parity to sustained advantage**: Value without rarity yields parity; rarity without protection yields temporary advantage; organization determines appropriation.

## Key Concepts

- **Resource heterogeneity**: Firms possess different resource bundles.
- **Imperfect mobility**: Resources cannot be traded or transferred without loss or cost.
- **Causal ambiguity**: Rivals cannot identify how resources produce outcomes.
- **Appropriation**: Ability of the firm rather than employees, partners, or customers to capture created value.

## Mental Models

- Use **valuable to whom, against which rivals, in what environment?**
- Evaluate bundles and complementarities, not isolated assets.
- Explain persistence through an isolating mechanism, not the label “unique.”

## Anti-patterns

- **Declaring any strength strategic**.
- **Inferring value from past performance** and creating circular logic.
- **Ignoring resource erosion and environmental change**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A retailer attributes advantage to its data. Rivals can buy similar data, so rarity is weak. Advantage instead comes from a socially complex bundle: store routines, experimentation culture, supplier integration, and decision rights. The research claim must specify that bundle and how it produces and protects value.

## Key Takeaways

1. Define the competitive comparison and value mechanism.
2. Separate asset possession, deployment capability, and value capture.
3. Identify a credible barrier to imitation or substitution.
4. Use dynamic capabilities to explain renewal under change.
