# Chapter 28: Self-Determination Theory

## Core Idea

Motivation varies in quality, not only quantity; autonomy, competence, and relatedness support internalization, sustained performance, learning, and well-being.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Basic psychological needs**: Autonomy is volition, competence is effective mastery, and relatedness is mutual connection.
- **Motivation continuum**: Amotivation → external regulation → introjected regulation → identified regulation → integrated regulation → intrinsic motivation.
- **Internalization**: Social contexts can help externally prompted behavior become personally endorsed.
- **Cognitive evaluation**: Rewards and feedback affect intrinsic motivation through their informational versus controlling meaning.

## Key Concepts

- **Autonomous motivation**: Acting with willingness and endorsement.
- **Controlled motivation**: Acting under external pressure or internal compulsion.
- **Need support**: Choice within structure, optimal challenge, informative feedback, and interpersonal care.
- **Need thwarting**: Active obstruction of autonomy, competence, or relatedness.

## Mental Models

- Use **why is the person acting?**, not merely whether motivation is high.
- Combine autonomy support with clear structure; autonomy is not absence of standards.
- Diagnose whether a reward informs competence or controls behavior.

## Anti-patterns

- **Equating autonomy with independence**.
- **Assuming all extrinsic motivation is harmful**.
- **Offering choice without resources, rationale, or competence support**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A manager mandates a new safety routine. A controlling message stresses punishment; an autonomy-supportive approach explains the rationale, invites input on implementation, provides training, and acknowledges inconvenience while retaining the safety standard. Compliance may become identified and more persistent.

## Key Takeaways

1. Measure motivation regulation and need satisfaction separately.
2. Design work to support all three needs.
3. Consider internalization when behavior must initially be required.
4. Distinguish need frustration from simple low satisfaction.
