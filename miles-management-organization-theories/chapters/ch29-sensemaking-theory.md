# Chapter 29: Sensemaking Theory

## Core Idea

When events disrupt expectations, people notice cues, link them to prior frames, enact aspects of the environment, and construct plausible stories that enable coordinated action.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Cue–frame connection**: Current cues gain meaning through frames developed from prior experience and socialization.
- **Seven properties**: Sensemaking is identity-grounded, retrospective, enactive, social, ongoing, cue-focused, and driven by plausibility more than accuracy.
- **Enactment–selection–retention**: Action creates data; interpretations are selected; workable accounts are retained.
- **Sensegiving**: Actors attempt to influence how others interpret events.

## Key Concepts

- **Extracted cue**: A noticed fragment treated as meaningful.
- **Frame**: Prior schema, value, rule, or story used to interpret cues.
- **Equivocality**: Presence of multiple plausible meanings.
- **Plausibility**: Sufficient coherence and credibility to support action.

## Mental Models

- Use **what's the story here, and what action does it enable?**
- Study talk, labels, meetings, artifacts, and action sequences—not just private cognition.
- Treat action and interpretation as recursive.

## Anti-patterns

- **Using sensemaking as a synonym for understanding**.
- **Assuming retrospective accounts are objective records**.
- **Treating leaders as sole authors of meaning**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

After a near-miss, engineers call it random noise while operators call it evidence of systemic overload. Each selects different cues and actions. A cross-functional reconstruction compares timelines, surfaces identity threats, and tests alternative stories through safe experiments. The selected account reorganizes future attention.

## Key Takeaways

1. Identify interruption, cues, frames, interactions, and resulting action.
2. Trace meaning temporally and socially.
3. Distinguish sensemaking from sensegiving.
4. Assess plausibility and practical consequences while retaining an evidence check.
