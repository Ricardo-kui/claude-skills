# Chapter 36: Social Network Theory

## Core Idea

Actors' opportunities and behavior depend on patterns of ties; position, connectivity, cohesion, and brokerage shape information, influence, control, and constraint.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Node–tie–network specification**: Define actors, relation type, direction, weight, boundary, and time.
- **Centrality**: Degree captures direct connections, closeness captures distance to others, betweenness captures brokerage, and eigenvector-like measures capture ties to prominent actors.
- **Strong and weak ties**: Strong ties support trust and complex transfer; weak ties often bridge otherwise separated clusters.
- **Structural holes and closure**: Brokerage provides novel information and control; closure supports enforcement and coordination.
- **Whole versus ego networks**: Whole-network designs map a bounded population; ego designs map focal actors' local ties.

## Key Concepts

- **Homophily**: Similar actors are more likely to connect.
- **Contagion**: States or behaviors spread through ties.
- **Selection**: Actors choose similar partners, creating resemblance without influence.
- **Network boundary**: Rule determining who and which ties enter analysis.

## Mental Models

- Use **structure before attribute** when relations plausibly shape behavior.
- Separate influence from homophilous selection.
- Match network metric to mechanism; centrality is not one construct.

## Anti-patterns

- **Using convenient communication data as the theoretical network**.
- **Calling correlation among connected actors contagion**.
- **Interpreting every high-centrality position as beneficial**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A safety practice appears to spread through advice ties. Longitudinal data show that employees also form ties with already similar peers. A stochastic or panel design separates tie formation from behavioral influence. Betweenness matters only if brokers actually transmit credible safety information.

## Key Takeaways

1. Define relation, boundary, direction, weight, and time.
2. Tie each metric to an explicit mechanism.
3. Address dependence and network endogeneity.
4. Distinguish network structure from resources available through it.
