# Chapter 38: Structural Contingency Theory

## Core Idea

No organizational structure is universally best; effectiveness depends on fit among structure, strategy, size, technology, environment, and other contingencies.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Contingency fit**: The effect of structure on performance changes with the level of a contingency.
- **Mechanistic–organic continuum**: Formalization and centralization fit stable, analyzable tasks differently from uncertain, changing tasks.
- **Information-processing view**: As uncertainty rises, organizations require greater capacity through lateral relations, decentralization, or integrative roles.
- **Fit forms**: Congruence, interaction/contingency, profile deviation, and holistic configuration require different theory and measurement.
- **Adaptation cycle**: Contingency change produces misfit, performance pressure, structural adaptation, and renewed fit.

## Key Concepts

- **Structure**: Distribution of authority, specialization, formalization, and coordination.
- **Contingency**: Contextual variable changing the value of a structural arrangement.
- **Misfit**: Incompatibility between structure and contextual demands.
- **Equifinality**: Multiple configurations may produce comparable effectiveness.

## Mental Models

- Use **structure for which task and environment?**
- State the functional form of fit before choosing a statistical test.
- Treat adaptation as delayed and costly rather than automatic.

## Anti-patterns

- **Adding an interaction term and calling it contingency theory** without a fit rationale.
- **Assuming maximal flexibility is always optimal**.
- **Defining fit after observing performance**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A centralized retailer performs well in a stable national market but enters heterogeneous regions requiring local response. Decentralization increases information-processing capacity but risks inconsistency. Integrative systems and selective decision rights create a configuration fitted to uncertainty rather than a universal shift toward decentralization.

## Key Takeaways

1. Specify structure, contingency, fit form, and outcome.
2. Distinguish selection, interaction, and profile-deviation models.
3. Model adjustment costs and temporal lag.
4. Compare efficiency fit with institutional conformity.
