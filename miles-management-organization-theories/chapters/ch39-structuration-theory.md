# Chapter 39: Structuration Theory

## Core Idea

Structure and agency are a duality: rules and resources enable and constrain action, while recurring knowledgeable action reproduces or changes those same structures.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Duality of structure**: Structure is both medium and outcome of practice, not an external object independent of actors.
- **Three modalities**:
  - Interpretive schemes connect signification with communication.
  - Facilities connect domination with power.
  - Norms connect legitimation with sanction.
- **Practical and discursive consciousness**: Actors know how to act routinely and can articulate only part of that knowledge.
- **Time–space distanciation**: Social systems extend practices across distance and time through rules and resources.

## Key Concepts

- **Structure**: Virtual rules and resources instantiated in practice.
- **System**: Reproduced pattern of social relations.
- **Agency**: Capacity to act otherwise and make a difference.
- **Routinization**: Repetition that stabilizes social life and practical security.

## Mental Models

- Use **this practice draws on which rule/resource, and what does repetition reproduce?**
- Trace interaction over time instead of treating structure and behavior as independent variables only.
- Look for unintended consequences and reflexive monitoring.

## Anti-patterns

- **Treating structure as a static container**.
- **Claiming agency simply because change occurred**.
- **Using structuration language without tracing practices, modalities, and reproduction**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

Employees bypass a formal approval system through a shared spreadsheet. The workaround draws on informal interpretive schemes, access to data, and norms of responsiveness. Repetition legitimizes the workaround and redistributes power; later policy codifies it. Action both used and changed structure.

## Key Takeaways

1. Identify recurrent practices and their rules and resources.
2. Connect communication, power, and sanction to specific modalities.
3. Analyze reproduction and transformation longitudinally.
4. Preserve actor knowledgeability without assuming full awareness or control.
