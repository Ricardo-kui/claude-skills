# Chapter 41: Conclusion

## Core Idea

Master theory by moving from recognition to application, comparison, evaluation, and creation; select theories by phenomenon, mechanism, level, and boundary, then develop contributions through disciplined engagement with the relevant literature.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Six learning levels**: Remember → understand → apply → analyze → evaluate → create.
- **Theory-selection workflow**: Choose an important phenomenon, identify candidate mechanisms, compare theories, immerse in seminal and recent work, and map constructs, relationships, evidence, criticisms, and boundaries.
- **Ten contribution moves**: Review, clarify, replicate, extend/elaborate, reformulate, criticize, borrow, blend, integrate, or refocus.
- **Theory-development stages**: Phenomenon/tension → search → elaboration and research → proclamation/presentation.
- **Theorist roles**: Creator, codifier, disseminator, researcher, and advocate.

## Key Concepts

- **Level of analysis**: Focal unit at which constructs and relationships operate.
- **Cross-level theory**: Explanation linking constructs at different levels with an explicit aggregation or emergence mechanism.
- **Fact and foil**: Ask why observed P occurred rather than plausible Q.
- **Allomorph**: Generate alternatives by shifting which element of a question receives emphasis.

## Mental Models

- Use **phenomenon before theory**; a favored theory should not dictate what counts as a problem.
- Compare candidate theories on mechanism and scope, not citation popularity.
- Treat integration as earned: combine theories only when each explains a distinct, necessary part of the causal story.

## Anti-patterns

- **Name-dropping multiple theories** without resolving overlap or contradiction.
- **Level hopping**: Moving from individual constructs to organizational outcomes without composition logic.
- **Extending by adding variables** without changing explanation.
- **Creating novelty by relabeling established constructs**.

## Constructed Application

> Synthetic application derived from the theory; not a quotation or documented case from Miles (2012).

A researcher observes that firms copy competitors during crises. Institutional theory predicts mimetic conformity under uncertainty; social network theory predicts exposure through ties; sensemaking theory predicts cue–frame construction; diffusion theory predicts staged adoption. A strong project selects or sequences mechanisms based on observed process and level instead of listing all four as background.

## Key Takeaways

1. State the phenomenon, level, mechanism, and boundary before choosing a theory.
2. Immerse in current literature; this book is a launch point, not the contemporary frontier.
3. Make a contribution by changing explanation, scope, evidence, or integration—not vocabulary alone.
4. Preserve distinctions among theory, construct, proposition, variable, and measure.
