# Glossary

**Absorptive capacity** — Capacity to recognize, assimilate, transform, and exploit external knowledge (Ch01).

**Actant** — Human or nonhuman entity that makes a difference within an actor-network (Ch02).

**Agency costs** — Monitoring, bonding, and residual losses arising from delegation (Ch03).

**Asset specificity** — Loss of value when an asset is redeployed outside a focal transaction (Ch40).

**Attribution** — Perceived causal explanation for an outcome or behavior (Ch06).

**Autonomous motivation** — Action experienced as volitional and personally endorsed (Ch28).

**Behavioral intention** — Readiness to perform a behavior defined by action, target, context, and time (Ch23).

**Boundary condition** — Condition delimiting where, when, or for whom a mechanism operates (Ch00).

**Brokerage** — Position connecting otherwise separated actors or clusters (Ch30, Ch36).

**Capability** — Patterned capacity to deploy resources or perform an organizational task (Ch10, Ch26).

**Causal ambiguity** — Uncertainty about how a resource bundle produces performance (Ch26).

**Construct** — Abstract concept serving as a theoretical building block (Ch00).

**Decoupling** — Separation of formal adopted structure from operational practice (Ch17).

**Dynamic capability** — Capacity to purposefully create, extend, or modify a resource base (Ch10).

**Equivocality** — Presence of multiple plausible meanings, not merely missing information (Ch19, Ch29).

**Institutional isomorphism** — Similarity produced by coercive, mimetic, or normative pressures (Ch17).

**Legitimacy** — Generalized perception that conduct is appropriate within institutional norms (Ch17).

**Loss aversion** — Greater psychological impact of losses than equivalent gains (Ch24).

**Mental model** — Simplified internal representation of how a system works (Ch20).

**Network centrality** — Family of positional measures capturing different forms of connectivity or brokerage (Ch36).

**Organizational justice** — Perceived fairness of outcomes, procedures, treatment, and explanations (Ch22).

**Psychological contract** — Individual belief about reciprocal promises and obligations (Ch25).

**Reference point** — Standard relative to which an outcome is coded as a gain or loss (Ch24).

**Role conflict** — Incompatibility among expectations attached to one or more roles (Ch27).

**Self-efficacy** — Task-specific belief in capability to execute required action (Ch31).

**Sensemaking** — Social process of linking extracted cues to frames to support plausible action (Ch29).

**Social capital** — Resources embedded in and available through relationships (Ch30).

**Social identity** — Self-definition derived from group membership (Ch35).

**Stakeholder salience** — Attention generated by power, legitimacy, and urgency (Ch37).

**Structural fit** — Compatibility between organizational structure and contingencies (Ch38).

**Structuration** — Recursive production of social systems through rules, resources, and practice (Ch39).

**Transaction cost** — Cost of searching, contracting, monitoring, adapting, and enforcing exchange (Ch40).

**Variable** — Operational representation used to observe or test a construct (Ch00).
