# Theory Reasoning Patterns

Read only the contract matching the request, then use the reusable patterns required by that contract. The `anchor test` in `SKILL.md` remains the completion gate for research-facing theory selection.

## Theory-Selection Contract

Return: phenomenon and level; 2–4 candidates; anchor theory; closest rival; non-interchangeable mechanisms; boundaries; distinguishing evidence; epistemic split.

## Hypothesis-Development Contract

Return:

1. Construct definitions and levels.
2. Trigger → mechanism → intermediate state → outcome chain.
3. Directional hypothesis or proposition.
4. Boundary or moderator with a mechanism-based rationale.
5. Closest rival prediction and distinguishing evidence.
6. Measurement implications kept separate from construct definitions.
7. `frontier check` status.

## Organizational-Diagnosis Contract

Return: observed symptom; theory-based mechanism; competing diagnosis; intervention target; likely side effects; contextual limits; evidence that would revise the diagnosis. Present managerial action as a conditional implication rather than as proof of the theory.

## Named-Theory Study Contract

Return: core idea; constructs; mechanism; unit and boundaries; strongest application; major anti-pattern; closest adjacent theories. Include source anchors only when requested or needed for academic use.

## What–How–Why–Boundary Audit

**Use when**: Evaluating a conceptual model or hypothesis.

**Apply**: Name constructs; specify relation form; state generative mechanism; define actor, level, context, and time; then select measures.

**Trade-off**: More boundaries improve precision but reduce claimed generality.

## Candidate-Theory Tournament

**Use when**: Several theories could explain the same outcome.

**Apply**: For every candidate, record unit, mechanism, distinctive mediator, predicted process, boundary, and disconfirming observation. Select one anchor or sequence complementary mechanisms.

**Trade-off**: Parsimony can omit real multimechanism dynamics; integration can become incoherent.

## Mechanism Chain

**Use when**: Turning a broad association into an explanation.

**Apply**: Trigger → actor interpretation or capability → action or interaction → intermediate state → outcome. State competing pathways at each uncertain link.

**Trade-off**: Detailed chains require stronger temporal evidence.

## Level-of-Analysis Check

**Use when**: Linking individual, group, organization, field, or population constructs.

**Apply**: Assign a level to every construct; state composition, emergence, or cross-level influence; align measurement and sampling.

**Trade-off**: Multilevel realism increases design and estimation burden.

## Theory-Contribution Ladder

**Use when**: Designing a contribution after completing the `frontier check`.

**Apply**: Choose among review, clarify, replicate, extend, reformulate, criticize, borrow, blend, integrate, or refocus. State what explanation changes and why that change matters.

**Trade-off**: Larger moves demand stronger command of the frontier and rival accounts.
