## Story Reading

### Theme question

Before an anticipated recall becomes public, should a firm increase or decrease advertising to protect shareholder value, when that same adjustment can signal confidence or make the later quality failure more disappointing?

### Whole-story synopsis

The paper begins with the familiar stock-price damage of a recall but shifts attention to a preventive marketing choice available while firms often know a recall is pending. Prerecall advertising becomes the central ambiguous action. Its increase can signal that the firm privately expects limited damage to future cash flow, but it can also raise product-quality expectations and sharpen disappointment when a defect is revealed. A decrease reverses those effects: it can signal deeper trouble while potentially lowering expectations. Product newness governs the informational value of the signal because new products leave outsiders with less public information; hazard severity governs the magnitude of expectation violation. The result is a conditional map rather than a universal advertising prescription. Automobile event-study evidence shows that increasing ads cushions the return loss for new products with minor hazards but worsens it for old products with major hazards; it has no net effect for new products with major hazards. Decreasing ads hurts new-product recalls under both hazard levels and does not produce the predicted benefit for old-major recalls. Robustness exercises preserve the central pattern. The conclusion then transforms a recommendation to cut advertising for short-run profit into a marketing–finance trade-off: a spending reduction can be privately economical yet worsen shareholders' announcement response.

### Characters and storylines

- **Crisis character:** the impending product recall, which is privately anticipated before announcement and publicly resolves uncertainty about a quality defect.
- **Main action character:** an unexpected increase or decrease in advertising for the recalled product before disclosure.
- **Signal character:** investor inference from advertising about the firm's private assessment of defect severity and future cash flow.
- **Expectation character:** the higher quality expectations created by increased advertising and the disappointment when a major hazard violates them.
- **Boundary characters:** product newness identifies high versus low public information; hazard severity identifies weak versus strong expectation violation.
- **Storyline 1:** higher prerecall advertising → confidence signal → less stock-value damage, especially for a new product with a minor hazard.
- **Storyline 2:** higher prerecall advertising → higher quality expectations → greater disappointment and damage, especially for an older product with a major hazard.
- **Intersection:** the same action changes investor valuation through opposing routes whose relative strength is inferred from the recall's information environment and hazard.

### Five acts

- **Exposition:** Recalls reliably hurt firm value, while firms often have time before disclosure to alter advertising and need a preventive crisis-management tool.
- **Rising action:** Advertising is given a positive signal role and a negative expectation role; newness and hazard explain why their relative strength should vary.
- **Climax:** Announcement-window returns reveal the predicted positive effect of advertising increases for new-minor recalls and negative effect for old-major recalls, alongside the harmful effects of decreases for new-product recalls.
- **Falling action:** The non-significant new-major and old-major-decrease cells refine the conditional map; alternative windows, thresholds, fixed effects, IV, and placebo windows test its stability.
- **Denouement:** The Discussion turns the findings into an integrated marketing–finance choice, not a general rule to spend more or less before every recall.

### Tension

- **Source:** An advertising increase can be a reassuring costly signal of future strength and the very action that makes a quality defect feel like a sharper breach of expectation.
- **Construction:** The paper assigns product newness to the information-asymmetry route and hazard to the disappointment route, which makes the direction of the same action conditional rather than arbitrary.

### Alternative readings

- **author-signaled-alternative:** The paper reports that positive and negative effects can cancel for new-major recalls and that decreasing advertising does not create the expected positive expectation effect for old-major recalls, suggesting asymmetry between raising and lowering expectations.
- **analyst_counterfactual:** Advertising adjustments may reflect unobserved recall severity, product demand, or firm condition rather than a signal investors interpret. Fixed effects, competitor-advertising IV, and placebo windows address parts of this concern, but investor beliefs are unobserved.

- **Character discipline:** `works` — signal and expectation roles are distinct, while newness and hazard identify different conditions for their salience.
- **Knot integrity:** `works` — a marketing action that can both protect and worsen shareholder value is a genuine decision problem.

## Learning Affordances

### Introduction and Theory

Use this card when one visible pre-event action can reasonably generate two opposed interpretations after hidden information is disclosed, and separate conditions diagnose the strength of each interpretation. It is not a generic rationale for treating every moderation effect as a signal-versus-expectation paradox.

### Methods, Results, and Discussion

The card shows how conditional cells can carry the main result when an average action effect cancels. Its calibration is `not_directly_tested`: advertising deviations and market reactions are observed, not investor inference or disappointment.

## Comparison prompt
