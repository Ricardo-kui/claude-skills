## Story Reading

### Theme question

What constrains adaptation in vertical relationships when there is no incentive conflict to blame — and do the two flagship constructs of organization design, differentiation and integration, explain performance differences across make, buy, and ally that the governance-form-versus-transaction-hazard match cannot?

### Whole-story synopsis

The paper opens inside its rival's house: vertical procurement is transaction cost economics' favorite empirical domain, and the field reads adaptation through one lens — incentive conflict, hold-up, opportunistic renegotiation. The knot is tied with the founder's own thread. Williamson defined adaptation as the theory's core problem and himself conceded that adaptation failures may persist "even in the absence of incentive conflict," yet that recognition "finds scant recognition" in the empirical literature the theory motivated. The knot is a half-domain gap wearing an overlooked-alternative face: adaptation naturally decomposes into cooperative adjustment (alignment of interests) and coordinated adjustment (alignment of actions), and the entire empirical tradition — including the relational-norms critics, as the conclusion will show — has institutionalized only the cooperative half. The paper's move is to broaden, not to battle: it imports the Barnard lineage's constructs, differentiation and integration, from intra-organizational design into vertical relationships between and within firms, defines the adaptive capacity of a relationship as their joint product, and promises a fit story — performance depends on the match between a mode's adaptive capacity and the adaptation requirements of the exchange, "not only the match between governance form and transaction hazards."

The middle builds the engine in two strokes. First the portraits: modes differ systematically in the two attributes (H1: external modes are more differentiated, because intra-firm conformity pressures cap differentiation; H2: make and ally are more integrated, because coordination mechanisms — programming, hierarchy, feedback, shared experience, alliance routines — are unavailable at arm's length), fixed in Figure 1 as make = low differentiation/high integration, buy = the reverse, ally = balanced. Second the pressures: transaction instability demands responsiveness (requisite variety, parallel search — differentiation's job), reciprocal task interdependence demands coordination (integration's job), and jointly they are superadditive, which is where the balanced profile should pay. The cooperation/coordination decomposition is the hinge that makes the whole story coherent: incentives, sanctions, and monitoring solve cooperation but "are not sufficient to achieve coordination," so governance modes must be compared as modes of organization, not just incentive systems. Three fit hypotheses follow with no unconditional performance claims anywhere — the paper never says which mode wins, only which pressure hurts which portrait most (H3 instability punishes make; H4 interdependence punishes buy; H5 joint pressures punish ally least). The empirical arena then pre-commits to the theory's shape: a transaction-level survey of Ford and Chrysler procurement (222 relationships) measures the portraits directly, and — because modes are chosen, not assigned — a switching regression that explicitly refuses the naive pooled-interaction model makes the fit test credible against self-selection. The verdict arrives in promised order: the portraits hold (H1 strongly, H2 supported, with the alliance's integration landing below internal procurement); instability hurts make alone (H3 supported); H4 is honestly not supported (buy does not do significantly worse than ally under interdependence); and the instability-by-interdependence interaction is positive and significant only for ally (H5 supported) — the balanced hybrid earns its keep precisely when both pressures co-occur. Supplier asset specificity still pays in make and ally, the TCE rival's variables working alongside, not against. The ending transforms the opening: adaptation "is not only cooperative adjustment; it is also coordinated adjustment"; the Barnard tradition is revived between organizations; instability is split from volume uncertainty and interdependence from asset specificity so the perspective is complementary to, not a relabeling of, TCE; the contribution is restated at debate level ("when is each explanation more useful"); the fit logic is compressed into a 2x2 optimal-mode table; and the paper confesses its own best puzzle — if alliances adapt best under joint pressure, why isn't everything an alliance? — answering with a labeled speculation about setup costs.

### Characters and storylines

- **Main character — the vertical procurement relationship (as make, buy, or ally):** the unit of analysis and the comparative protagonist set; the story tracks three organizing modes through a two-dimensional trait space, and the plot question is which portrait survives which weather.
- **Main construct — adaptive capacity (differentiation × integration):** the paper's transplant and its title contribution; it converts "modes of governance" into "profiles of organization," which is what lets performance claims become conditional at all.
- **Supporting character — differentiation:** the responsiveness engine (requisite variety, parallel search); its scarcity defines make's vulnerability to instability.
- **Supporting character — integration, internally split into cooperation and coordination:** the alignment engine and the story's conceptual hinge; the assertion that incentives buy cooperation but not coordination is what makes the missing half of adaptation a real half rather than a rewording.
- **Supporting characters — the two adaptation pressures:** transaction instability and reciprocal task interdependence; the demand side of the fit story, cast as weather systems that each punish a different portrait, with their joint occurrence crowning the balanced mode.
- **Dignified rival — asset specificity / hold-up:** never a straw man; it enters the design as controls, keeps effects in the results (supplier specificity pays in make and ally), and is met in the Discussion with a dimension split rather than dismissal.
- **Offstage anchor — Williamson:** the founder whose broad sentences license the departure; he functions as the story's authority for broadening rather than as an opponent.
- **Storyline 1 (portraits):** H1/H2 fix each mode's position on differentiation and integration; resolved in Table 3 with a nuance that matters later (ally's integration is below make's).
- **Storyline 2 (fit under single pressure):** H3 and H4 run the two pressures against the portraits; one resolves for the theory (H3), one honestly fails (H4) — the story's tensest beat.
- **Storyline 3 (joint pressure and complementarity):** H5's superadditivity argument makes differentiation and integration complements and gives the balanced ally its unique claim; it also absorbs the H4 failure (small integration differences leveraged by complementarity).
- **Intersection:** all three storylines hang on one hinge — the cooperation/coordination decomposition — and all are steered by the same discipline: no unconditional performance claims, so the story can only win as a pattern of conditional effects.

### Five acts

- **Exposition:** Vertical relationships as TCE's home turf; the founder's broad adaptation problem quoted against the field's narrowed hold-up practice; two-turn problematization (field-level Yet, proposition-level However); the goal sentence ("beyond incentive conflict") lands at the end of the first paragraph, and the organization-design lens is imported as the broadened construct's definition.
- **Rising action:** The 1937 double genealogy (Coase/Barnard) positions the import as reunion of twin traditions; integration is decomposed into cooperation and coordination ("a distinction seldom maintained"); H1/H2 anchor the portraits with mechanism inventories; Figure 1 locks the shared premise mid-section; H3/H4/H5 derive as pure conditional predictions under the fit 总纲; Methods builds the arena — 37 interviews, expert-informant verification, transaction-level measurement, and the switching-regression announcement with its three-part causal-language limiters and prospective exclusion restriction.
- **Climax:** The staged reveal in promised order — portraits first (Table 3), then the fit verdicts: instability significant and negative only for make (H3); interdependence marginally positive for make, zero for buy and ally, and the explicit verdict "Hypothesis 4 is not supported"; the centered interaction positive and significant only for ally (H5) — the balanced hybrid's complementarity payoff, the story's highest point, arriving immediately after its most honest defeat.
- **Falling action:** Side payments that broaden without derailing — the first-stage multinomial read "for its own merit" (alliances protect supplier-specific investments; buyer and buyer-side monopsony asymmetry), all labeled exploratory; supplier specificity effects consistent with hold-up reported alongside; the selection threat closed on the double signal (null hazard, significant Hausman); VIF and robust-SE hygiene in the descriptive paragraph.
- **Denouement:** The Discussion returns to the opening sentence and transforms it — adaptation is cooperative and coordinated adjustment; the Barnard tradition is revived between organizations; instability is split from volume uncertainty (with the TCE-opposite signed prediction made explicit) and interdependence from asset specificity; three contributions culminate in the debate-level reframe ("when certain explanations ... are more useful"); Table 6 compresses the fit logic into an optimal-mode map; the alliance-prevalence puzzle is confessed and speculated on; the Conclusion lands the reframe — "different governance modes are also different modes of organization."

### Tension

- **Source:** The challenge is internal to a paradigm the authors intend to broaden rather than overthrow: the empirical apparatus of a dominant theory has drifted from its founder's own formulation, and the broadening must be licensed, precise (which constraint class is missing?), and testable — or it reads as relabeling. A second, quieter tension: a fit story that refuses to name an overall winning mode stakes everything on conditional patterns, so any failed condition (H4) is visible.
- **Construction:** The founder's sentences do the licensing; the two-turn problematization does the narrowing; the cooperation/coordination decomposition does the precision work ("incentives ... are not sufficient to achieve coordination"); the fit 总纲 and portrait summary do the expectation management; the switching-regression refusal of the naive model does the credibility work; and the honest H4 verdict, unsoftened, does the trust work — the paper wins more from losing one hypothesis cleanly than it would have from five supports.

### Alternative readings

- **author_signaled_alternative:** A pure TCE reading predicts internal procurement advantaged under uncertainty (volume unpredictability as hazard), the opposite of H3; the paper signals this head-on in the Discussion and footnotes David & Han's (2004) review finding no clear uncertainty-governance relationship — the discriminating prediction is offered, not hidden.
- **author_signaled_alternative:** The "why aren't all external relationships alliances?" puzzle invites a cost-side reading (setup costs of each mode) that the paper itself raises and explicitly leaves outside its analysis.
- **analyst_counterfactual:** The paper could be read as paradigms-at-war adjudication (TCE vs. adaptation perspective) rather than complementary broadening; the authors forestall this by keeping asset-specificity effects alive in their own models and closing with "when is each explanation more useful." Recorded here as analyst-generated because the complementarity framing is the paper's own.
- **analyst_counterfactual:** The H4 failure might partly reflect measurement: the integration scale blends cooperation and coordination items, so the coordination half that H4 needs cannot be detected separately. The L2 theory distillation records this blend as an honest boundary; this card extends it into an alternative explanation of the null.

## Learning Affordances

### Introduction

- **Suitable:** `yes`
- **Learn:** (1) Founder-licensed broadening — let the paradigm's own canonical text authorize the departure, then narrow in two turns from field practice to the omitted proposition class, so the contribution reads as return-to-founders rather than insurrection; state the goal in a "beyond X" sentence immediately. (2) The beyond/not-only ring — mirror the opening goal in the preview's final sentence ("arise as a function of the match between [new capacity] and [new requirement], and not only the match between [incumbent] and [incumbent hazard]"), making one sentence do preview, contribution contract, and closure with zero overclaim.
- **Do not copy:** Requires a founder whose broad formulation is verbatim quotable and genuinely wider than the field's operationalization (if empirical work has already followed the founder, "scant recognition" is unusable); the zero-human-face, zero-stakes, no-explicit-RQ economistic style is 2005 SMJ-native (L2 risks nl1/nl2) — modern outlets need an actor, a stakes sentence, and an RQ variant.

### Theory

- **Suitable:** `yes`
- **Learn:** (1) Same-era dual-lineage opening for a construct transplant — stage the borrowing as reuniting two twin traditions born the same year, then declare the extension target ("We develop and extend this tradition ... to the inter-organizational context") so complementarity is structural, not rhetorical. (2) Anchor-then-branch architecture — fix testable portraits first (H1/H2), lock them with a mid-section summary and a 2x2-style figure, then derive pure conditional branches that refer back to the portraits, with a fit 总纲 sentence up front and no unconditional performance claims anywhere.
- **Do not copy:** The L2 flag applies: this theory sits genuinely between mechanism-inventory and categorical-fit types — decide which engine is your contribution before imitating, or the section's 45% mechanism inventory will read as throat-clearing. The branch tree is only as strong as its anchor (if portraits fall, everything falls), and the cooperation/coordination hinge must be measured separately in any modern version, not asserted and then blended into one scale as here.

### Methods

- **Suitable:** `partial`
- **Learn:** Design-to-story alignment: the transaction-level unit upgrade declaration, the expert-informant qualification chain, and the switching-regression announcement (naive model rejected, correction attempted, hazard semantics stated, exclusion restriction declared prospectively) each answer a named objection inside the design chapter — the announcement's three-part causal-language limiter is the transferable rhythm.
- **Do not copy:** The 2005 validity stack (alpha+AGFI only, unquantified category corroboration, single informant, no attrition funnel, no setting paragraph) is era artifact; a modern version needs the full measurement-validity stack, clustering/funnel reporting, and a mechanism argument for the exclusion restriction — borrow the announcement rhythm, not the stack.

### Results

- **Suitable:** `yes`
- **Learn:** (1) Portrait-before-adjudicate sequencing with auditable comparison — stage construct-level portraits before performance verdicts, name the exact column subset per hypothesis, and give cross-model claims a joint-covariance dual track so the reader can re-derive every verdict. (2) The honest-null double beat — verdict sentence without concession words, pattern restatement in hypothesis language, nulls used as comparison anchors, and the diagnostics close on a double signal (null correction term, significant Hausman) narrated with precision humility; this is the paper's most transferable results rhythm.
- **Do not copy:** Comparative verdicts here lean on bracketed significance rather than explicit between-model tests, subgroup-difference claims go unstatisticized, the make cell is N=20 undiscussed, and no magnitude is ever translated — a modern version must add the formal test and the economic-significance beat the 2005 grammar omits.

### Discussion

- **Suitable:** `yes`
- **Learn:** (1) Dimension-split boundary work — separate your constructs from the rival's nearest neighbors on defined dimensions, ideally with opposite signed predictions (instability vs. volume uncertainty; interdependence vs. asset specificity), converting "is this just relabeling?" into a testable split. (2) The debate-reframing close — upgrade contributions from findings level to debate level ("when is each explanation more useful"), compress the theory into a small decision map, and confess the residual puzzle your own theory creates, with speculation labeled as speculation.
- **Do not copy:** The reframe depends on a genuine live debate to move (hold-up vs. relational norms); the decision map inherits the paper's missing magnitude story and should not be drawn from sign comparisons alone; the long limitations catalogue would be restructured today.
