## Story Reading

### Theme question

Is the value of partnering experience a matter of how much experience a firm has, or of what kind — does experience accumulated with the same partner (PSE) create value where generalized experience across all prior alliances (GPE) does not, and under what conditions of opportunity, capacity, and information can a firm actually convert partner-specific experience into gains from a new alliance?

### Whole-story synopsis

The paper opens on a quantified paradox inside an accepted proposition: alliances have grown tremendously, yet success rates stay low, and the market evidence is stark — only about half of alliance participants obtain positive returns, and gains are marginally significant and low on average. The field's favored remedy, accumulated partnering experience, is "widely accepted" as a predictor of value creation, but its own empirical tests show mixed or limited support. Rather than discarding the proposition or adding another contingency, the paper questions its premise: maybe the field has been measuring the wrong kind of experience. A four-question cascade reframes the puzzle (distinctive types? enabling conditions? which firms? which partners?), the contribution is declared as premise reversal plus boundary conditions plus construct differentiation, and the design is previewed — alliance-formation events, abnormal stock returns around announcement, joint ventures among Fortune 300 firms, 1987-1996. The theory section builds the engine by splitting the construct: general partnering experience (GPE), the firm-level foundation of relational capability, versus partner-specific experience (PSE), the dyadic accumulation that builds trust and coordination with a particular partner. Two reasons make PSE the stronger character: learning from PSE is more efficient because it unfolds in a consistent context, and PSE buys richer benefits because many relational mechanisms — relation-specific assets, knowledge-sharing routines, mutual trust — simply do not travel to other partners. Hence H1 (PSE raises announcement returns) and the comparative H2 (PSE contributes more than GPE). PSE is then declared an insufficient condition, and three enhancers take the stage in parallel, each sourced from a different quarter: partner distinctiveness, which balances the "rich" interaction of recurrent ties with "reach" to novel learning opportunities (H3); the munificence of technological and financial resources, which gives the firm the capacity to apply what it learned, with trust reducing the fear of misappropriation (H4); and firm-specific uncertainty, under which investors starved of information lean harder on the dyadic track record as an informational clue (H5) — the lens quietly shifting from the learning firm to the evaluating market. The methods make the promise testable: bilateral JVs among Fortune 300 firms yield two firm-side observations per event, GPE and PSE are updated event-by-event as counts back to 1975, CAR is computed over a conservative two-day window, and the moderation battery lives in partial models whose full-model counterpart (VIF 34.1) is kept as a disclosed reference. The results stage the verdict as a sequence of paired judgments: the rival's null goes first and persists (GPE insignificant in Model 1 and every model after); PSE enters with a marginally significant positive effect — moderate support for H1 — and the section closes its first movement with the adjudication sentence, "PSE rather than GPE contributes to value creation in new alliances," upgrading the comparison with a one-sided F-test (H2). Each moderator then gets its own paired verdict: PSE×partner distinctiveness significant (H3); GPE×TFR null against PSE×TFR significant, with the PSE main effect honestly surrendered to its own interaction (H4, Model 4); GPE×FFR null against PSE×FFR significant, with the negative FFR main effect hedged-reread as financially distressed firms benefiting more (H4, Model 5); PSE×FSU significant against a null GPE×FSU that is recycled as confirmation of PSE's superiority (H5). Falling action polices the claims: null controls are anchored in prior literature, low R² is normalized by field benchmarks, and the discussion re-diagnoses the discrepant findings that opened the story — Hoang & Rothaermel's null becomes an outcome-measure and setting difference, Goerzen's negative PSE effect becomes low partner distinctiveness — turning the field's mixed record into boundary conditions of this paper's claim. The ending transforms the question: firms must balance familiarity with novelty, because experience and distinctiveness are "mutually reinforcing rather than mutually exclusive"; knowledge application joins the learning chain as its missing element; uncertainty not only motivates alliances but raises the value of recurrent ones; and the conclusion hands managers the coda that today's choice of alliance partners shapes the gains of tomorrow's.

### Characters and storylines

- **Main character — partner-specific experience (PSE):** the focal facet and identity claim of the paper; introduced as the construct the field had written off ("We question the premise that partner-specific experience offers limited value"), given a two-part mechanism (efficient learning in a consistent context; non-fungible relational benefits), a main effect (H1), a superiority claim (H2), all three moderation interactions (H3-H5), and a second life as an investor-facing signal under uncertainty.
- **Foil — general partnering experience (GPE):** the incumbent, carrying the field's accepted proposition; given its own entry in Model 1, a persistent null, parallel interaction terms in every moderator model, and nulls that are recycled as witnesses ("The insignificant interaction of GPE and FSU is consistent with our conjecture about the superiority of PSE over GPE"); the discussion partially rehabilitates it (foundation of firm-level relational capability) rather than executing it — a foil fired from the verdict but kept on the payroll.
- **Supporting cast — the three enhancers:** partner distinctiveness (novelty of learning opportunity, the richness-reach balance), operational resources TFR/FFR (application capacity, plus trust that de-risks committing resources), and firm-specific uncertainty (the informational scarcity that raises the value of the dyadic track record) — each with its own why-chain, each sourced from a different level (partner, firm, market).
- **Offstage audience — the investors:** never a measured construct, but the reason the story works; their information scarcity drives H5, their access to press releases justifies the design, and their reputation-based reading of CAR is the concession the limitations must make.
- **The mixed-findings record (Hoang & Rothaermel 2005; Goerzen 2007; Park & Kim 1997; Merchant & Schendel 2000; Anand & Khanna 2000):** not a personified opponent but the evidence pattern that opens the story and must be reconciled at the close — the paper's actual antagonist is a set of null and negative results, including its own foil's.
- **Storyline 1 (PSE rises):** distrusted dyadic artifact → differentiated construct with a mechanism → marginal positive effect → amplified under three conditions → signal under uncertainty.
- **Storyline 2 (GPE falls, then steadies):** accepted predictor → persistent null → nulls recycled as superiority confirmations → rehabilitated in the discussion as the foundation of relational capability.
- **Storyline 3 (the conditions):** main effect declared insufficient → three parallel enhancers from partner, firm, and market levels → each confirmed as an interaction, with the third re-aiming the story at investors.
- **Storyline 4 (reconciliation):** the field's mixed record → re-diagnosed on outcome measure, setting, and construct confound → converted into boundary conditions.
- **Intersection:** all four storylines meet at the alliance-announcement event — the point where a dyadic history becomes a market-readable signal, and where the firm's past with one partner is priced.

### Five acts

- **Exposition:** alliance growth against persistent failure; the market-evidence statistic (half of participants gain nothing significant); the accepted experience proposition and its mixed support; the premise question; the four-question cascade; contribution declared (rehabilitate PSE, map boundaries, differentiate the construct); design and data preview.
- **Rising action:** the theory section splits experience into GPE and PSE, grounds the split in learning context-specificity and non-fungible relational mechanisms (Toyota's suppliers as the emblem), derives H1 and the comparative H2, declares the main effect insufficient, and stages the three enhancers — partner distinctiveness (richness × reach), resource munificence (capacity + trust), firm-specific uncertainty (PSE as informational clue) — each closing in its own hypothesis.
- **Climax:** the paired verdicts of Table 2: GPE's persistent null; PSE's marginal positive entry and the "PSE rather than GPE" adjudication with the H2 F-test; PSE×PD significant; GPE×TFR null vs PSE×TFR significant (with the PSE main effect absorbed by its own interaction, disclosed); GPE×FFR null vs PSE×FFR significant (negative FFR main effect hedged-reread); PSE×FSU significant with the GPE×FSU null recycled as confirmation.
- **Falling action:** null controls anchored in prior literature; low R² normalized by field benchmarks; the discussion re-diagnoses Hoang & Rothaermel and Goerzen on outcome measure, setting, and construct confound, stating the comparison's limits in the same breath.
- **Denouement:** the contingencies fuse into the familiarity-with-novelty principle ("mutually reinforcing rather than mutually exclusive"); knowledge application named as the learning chain's missing element; uncertainty extended from motivator of alliances to raiser of recurrent ties' value; limitations concede the count proxy, the ex ante outcome, the JV-only setting, and the untested diminishing-returns branch; the conclusion lands on the practice coda — today's partner choice shapes tomorrow's gains.

### Tension

- **Source:** the challenge is an evidence pattern, not a rival school — the field's accepted proposition fails its own tests, and some evidence even points the other way (negative abnormal returns for recurrent alliances, negative experience effects, PSE harm under redundancy). An internal tension runs through the theory itself: the same familiarity that builds trust can breed opportunism and inertia, and the remedy (more recurrent ties) is bounded by diminishing returns the paper cannot test.
- **Construction:** the hook makes the failure quantified and public (half of participants gain nothing; gains marginal and low); the pivot sentence ("widely accepted... mixed or limited support") turns embarrassment into a premise question; the four-question cascade converts the field's scatter into an answerable agenda; and the results keep the tension audible by staging every verdict as a pair — the rival's silence and the protagonist's response in the same model — so the reader watches the adjudication happen rather than being told the conclusion.

### Alternative readings

- **author_signaled_alternative:** the discussion raises two readings it cannot test — that beyond some threshold PSE yields diminishing returns (the low mean PSE excuses the untested branch), and that positive CARs may reflect the market's favorable response to the partners' reputation "regardless of actual learning processes"; under the signaling reading the whole result is about investor perception, not learning — a reading the paper itself half-welcomes via H5 but never separates from the learning story.
- **analyst_counterfactual:** the paired-adjudication verdicts partly ride on measurement asymmetry — GPE averages 28.9 prior JVs (near-saturated incumbency) against PSE at 0.59 (rare events), so GPE's persistent null could reflect range restriction rather than theoretical inferiority; the H2 F-test is one-sided with modest F (2.83); and the H4 paired verdict in Model 4 sits next to a 0.894 correlation between the PSE main effect and its own interaction, a near-degenerate design. Read this way, the "A rather than B" cadence is stronger than its statistical spine — a possibility the era's conventions (standardized betas, no magnitude translation) leave unexamined.

## Learning Affordances

### Introduction

- **Suitable:** `yes`
- **Learn:** (1) Quantified-paradox hook aimed at the field's own accepted predictor — open on growth plus failure statistics, sharpen with the market-evidence fraction, then pivot on the accepted proposition's mixed support and convert the adequacy gap into a premise question that lets the contribution include rehabilitating a written-off construct. (2) Four-question cascade as RQ hinge — one question per funded hypothesis block (types, conditions, firms, partners), with a preview paragraph that walks definitions, the relative-order claim, and the contingency list in claim order, packing a complete argument into four paragraphs.
- **Do not copy:** the cascade scatters unless every question maps to a distinct funded hypothesis; the FSU contingency is previewed but missing from the cascade (a small asymmetry to close in any reuse); stakes embedded in the hook and contribution-before-preview are compact-intro idioms of the era, not a portable order.

### Theory

- **Suitable:** `yes`
- **Learn:** (1) Construct differentiation with dual rationale and comparative payoff — split the accepted construct, give each facet a distinct mechanism signature (firm-level capability vs dyadic trust plus non-fungible relational mechanisms), and push the split through to a relative-order hypothesis that the design can test coefficient-against-coefficient. (2) Insufficient-condition moderation battery — declare the main effect insufficient, then stage parallel enhancers each sourced from a different actor or level, letting the third quietly switch the audience (learning firm → evaluating investors) to widen the story without adding constructs.
- **Do not copy:** the battery presupposes uniformly amplifying contingencies — mixed-direction moderators break it; the mechanism is argued by literature parallel (Toyota, mode-specificity) rather than derived, so reuse requires a genuinely non-fungible component; and the signaling and learning readings coexist unforced — copying means inheriting the duty to keep them distinct or test between them.

### Methods

- **Suitable:** `partial`
- **Learn:** (1) Dyadic event-study plumbing as narrative infrastructure — two observations per event, event-updated experience counters, dual-source deduplication against original press releases, and a design note that press releases discuss prior ties, quietly certifying that the investor-side premise is observable. (2) Partial-models disclosure with the full model kept as a witness — report the collinear full model reference-only with its VIF and the reason, so the moderation battery's separate models read as design rather than selection.
- **Do not copy:** raw-count experience proxies (the very measure the theory criticizes), CAR as an ex ante outcome, the absence of magnitude translation, and era handling of dyadic dependence (weighting and year dummies instead of dyad-level errors) — all must be audited in a modern version, not inherited.

### Results

- **Suitable:** `yes`
- **Learn:** (1) Paired-adjudication verdict rhythm — stage the rival's null first and let it persist, deliver the focal effect against it, close with an "A rather than B" translation, upgrade to a relative-order F-test, and recycle the rival's interaction nulls as confirmations so the opponent's silence becomes testimony. (2) Interaction hygiene as candor beats — disclose the focal main effect absorbed by its own interaction, hedge-reread the unexpected sign (negative FFR → distressed-firms benefit), and anchor null controls in prior literature so low R² reads as field-normal.
- **Do not copy:** the rhythm outruns its statistical spine here — marginal flagship significance (p<.1), a one-sided F-test at F=2.83, standardized betas with no magnitude or economic-significance translation; a modern retelling must add amplitude and stronger inference before borrowing the cadence, and should check the GPE saturation/range-restriction alternative before accepting the paired verdicts at face value.

### Discussion

- **Suitable:** `yes`
- **Learn:** (1) Discrepancy reconciliation as boundary work — re-diagnose each discrepant prior finding on a named distinction (outcome measure, setting, construct confound), state the comparison's limits in the same paragraph, and thereby convert the field's mixed record into the boundary conditions of one's own claim. (2) Synthesis elevation with owned limits — fuse the contingencies into one portable principle (familiarity with novelty; mutually reinforcing rather than mutually exclusive), extend the home literature (uncertainty as value-amplifier), and concede the untested branches (diminishing returns, reputation alternative) explicitly in the limitations.
- **Do not copy:** the reconciliation asserts setting and measure differences it does not test — boundary work must not be presented as boundary evidence; the distressed-firm reading of the negative FFR effect is floated without follow-up; and the managerially portable principle is earned only because every contingency was tested — without that evidence the same synthesis is a platitude.
