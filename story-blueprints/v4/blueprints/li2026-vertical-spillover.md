## Story Reading

### Theme question

When an automaker announces a major product recall, do its suppliers lose firm value through a vertical spillover distinct from competitor contagion, and how do direct and extended network ties amplify or buffer that loss?

### Whole-story synopsis

The paper begins with a crisis spreading beyond the organization that triggered it, but redirects attention from the well-studied horizontal effect on competitors to the supplier firms bound to the buyer by specific interdependencies. That distinction matters because vertical spillover can arise not only from categorical association but also from disrupted future cash flows. Social network theory provides the unifying language: a buyer–supplier tie is a pipe through which operational disruptions travel and a prism through which outside audiences infer connected firms' quality, responsibility, and risk. The paper then extends the network beyond direct dyads. Common business ties make suppliers and buyers structurally similar and may intensify both shared disruption and guilt by association; common institutional owners and analysts can create shared information and interpretation that reduce ambiguity. A seven-source US automobile dataset pairs 154 media-reported major recalls with 752 buyer–supplier dyads. Event studies show a supplier CAR loss that arrives more slowly than the OEM's loss. Regression results show larger supplier losses with more common business ties and smaller losses with common institutional ownership and common analyst coverage. Analyst forecast downgrades and negative supplier media coverage serve as auxiliary evidence consistent with cash-flow and impression pathways. The Discussion recasts network embeddedness as conditionally double-edged: value-producing ties can become channels of both operational and evaluative harm in a crisis.

### Characters and storylines

- **Main character:** the buyer–supplier relationship, because it is the direct vertical conduit that makes a supplier exposed even when not identified as the source of a defect.
- **Outcome character:** supplier firm-value loss following a buyer's media-reported major recall, not the supplier's own recall or a competitor's sales response.
- **Pipe character:** cash-flow expectations, reflected in subsequent analyst sales and EPS forecast downgrades.
- **Prism character:** negative supplier media coverage, reflecting potential guilt-by-association evaluation.
- **Amplifier character:** common business ties, whose structural equivalence can intensify operational dependence and categorical linkage.
- **Buffer characters:** common institutional ownership and common analyst coverage, which are theorized to provide shared, more informed interpretation of risk.
- **Storyline 1:** a buyer recall disrupts expected supplier cash flows through supply-chain interdependence.
- **Storyline 2:** the same relationship invites stakeholders to attribute risk and quality problems across connected firms.
- **Intersection:** vertical spillover is stronger or weaker not merely because two firms are connected, but because their direct and extended network architecture changes what flows and what observers infer.

### Five acts

- **Exposition:** Crisis spillovers are consequential, but recall research has concentrated on horizontal competitor effects while neglecting suppliers' tie-specific exposure.
- **Rising action:** Networks as pipes and prisms integrate supply-chain risk propagation with categorization; business ties, common owners, and common analysts define amplifying or buffering extended-network conditions.
- **Climax:** Suppliers exhibit a significant negative CAR after major media-reported buyer recalls, with the supplier reaction emerging later than the OEM reaction.
- **Falling action:** Common business ties amplify supplier loss, whereas common institutional ownership and common analyst coverage attenuate it; analyst downgrades and negative media coverage give bounded evidence for the proposed pathways, followed by event-window and selection checks.
- **Denouement:** The paper turns direct and extended supply-network ties into a crisis-risk architecture and argues that embeddedness has a dark side under recall conditions.

### Tension

- **Source:** Supply-network ties usually create value through information, resources, and coordination, yet the same ties can transmit a buyer's recall loss to an operationally innocent supplier.
- **Construction:** The paper contrasts horizontal categorization with vertical interdependence and then shows that a single tie can generate both cash-flow and impression channels.

### Alternative readings

- **author-signaled-alternative:** Supplier CAR losses could reflect a general market reassessment of the buyer's prospects rather than a distinct cash-flow or reputational consequence for the supplier. Forecast downgrades and media-sentiment analyses are consistent with the two pathways, but neither directly observes disruption or stakeholder attribution.

## Story Assessment

- **Theme coherence:** `works` — vertical exposure, pipes and prisms, extended network conditions, event-study evidence, and the ending all address the same supplier-spillover question.
- **Character discipline:** `works` — direct ties, business-tie overlap, common owners, and common analysts are assigned distinguishable propagation or interpretation roles.
- **Knot integrity:** `works` — treating vertical spillover as merely a horizontal categorization effect leaves a real theoretical and practical omission in the recall literature.
- **Plot emergence:** `works` — the dyadic event study and extended-network conditions follow directly from the distinction between operational propagation and evaluative contagion.
- **Tie–unravel alignment:** `partly_works` — the paper establishes supplier value loss and the stated network associations, but the pipes and prisms mechanisms are supported with indirect auxiliary measures rather than observed in the same event process.
- **Ending quality:** `partly_works` — it meaningfully returns to embeddedness as a conditional liability, while prescriptive claims about stakeholder cultivation outrun the observed market reaction.
- **Boundary:** This evaluates storytelling only; it is not a judgment about causal identification, event-study validity, or research quality.

## Learning Affordances

### Introduction and Theory

This card is useful when a research question distinguishes an affected actor's direct relationship exposure from a broad category-based spillover and can explain why each has different transmission channels. It is not a generic reason to label every association among firms a network spillover.

### Methods and Results

The card shows how a second evidence arena can make a dual-pathway account more credible: market loss establishes the focal effect, while forecasts and media traces offer limited process checks. Those checks do not transform inferred channels into observed mechanisms.

### Discussion

The ending is a useful warning against treating beneficial network structure as monotonic. It should not be used to prescribe diversification or investor relations without evidence that those actions change the particular exposure at issue.
