## Story Reading

### Theme question

How can CEO stock options, ordinarily meant to align CEOs with shareholders, generate different impression-management choices during product recalls when current option wealth is at risk but prospective option wealth remains available?

### Whole-story synopsis

The paper starts from the conventional alignment promise of stock options and moves it into an adverse-event setting in which that promise becomes unstable. A medical-device recall threatens a CEO's already accumulated option value, yet the same option portfolio contains prospective gains that depend on the firm's longer-run credibility and growth. The paper makes these two wealth components its paired protagonists and identifies two communication tactics through which their opposed orientations may become visible: initiating a recall on a low-attention day and remaining silent about it in subsequent press releases. Behavioral agency and mixed-gamble theory supply the plot: current option wealth creates a preservation motive that favors short-term impression management, while prospective option wealth creates a future-oriented motive that reduces it. Negative media coverage is then cast not as a generic control but as a social monitor that deters concealment for the former and reinforces transparency for the latter. The evidence examines both tactics across FDA medical-device recalls. Results show the predicted positive current-wealth and negative prospective-wealth associations for low-attention initiation and strategic silence; the media patterns are clearer for silence than for timing. Robustness analyses test alternative identification approaches and distinguish the tactics. The Discussion returns to the alignment premise with a qualified reversal: option compensation can create stakeholder-harming information management unless boards and media discipline its short-term wealth-preservation side.

### Characters and storylines

- **Main characters:** current and prospective CEO option wealth, because the paper treats them as distinct temporal components of the same incentive instrument with opposed decision orientations.
- **Action characters:** strategic timing and strategic silence, which occur at different points in recall communication and make the mixed-gamble orientations observable.
- **Monitoring character:** negative media coverage, which raises the visibility and occupational cost of impression management.
- **Stake characters:** shareholders, patients, medical personnel, and regulators give recall communication consequences beyond the CEO's private wealth.
- **Storyline 1:** current option wealth makes an immediate price loss salient and is predicted to encourage low-attention initiation and silence.
- **Storyline 2:** prospective option wealth makes later gains salient and is predicted to discourage the same tactics.
- **Intersection:** media scrutiny changes both tracks but by different logics—deterrence of concealment versus reinforcement of long-term reputation building.

### Five acts

- **Exposition:** Stock options are normally portrayed as alignment devices, yet adverse product failures expose CEOs to acute downside risk and broad stakeholder consequences.
- **Rising action:** Mixed-gamble theory decomposes options into current and prospective wealth, then maps each orientation onto low-attention recall initiation and strategic silence; media scrutiny becomes the differentiating monitor.
- **Climax:** Current option wealth predicts more inattentive recalls and silence, whereas prospective option wealth predicts less of each tactic.
- **Falling action:** Media contingencies partly support the timing predictions and support the silence predictions; IV, selection, and CEO-fixed-effect analyses probe the associations and distinguish the two tactics.
- **Denouement:** The Discussion reframes option design and media scrutiny as safeguards against an incentive's short-term information-management consequences, while acknowledging that its later career and performance effects remain unobserved.

### Tension

- **Source:** The incentive meant to align CEOs with shareholder value can, during a crisis, make already accumulated wealth vulnerable and create a motive to manage attention rather than address stakeholder information needs.
- **Construction:** The paper makes the tension concrete by separating current option value from prospective value and by following recall communication from initiation timing to subsequent silence.

### Alternative readings

- **author-signaled-alternative:** The authors acknowledge that Friday recalls are not more frequent than other workday recalls and ask whether they reflect opportunism or may instead risk CEO credibility; the timing measure therefore cannot by itself establish strategic intent.

## Story Assessment

- **Theme coherence:** `works` — option-wealth decomposition, recall communication tactics, media scrutiny, and the Discussion all remain organized around crisis-time incentive alignment.
- **Character discipline:** `partly_works` — current and prospective wealth are clearly distinguished, but the two tactics receive uneven theoretical and empirical support and media becomes a broad additional character.
- **Knot integrity:** `works` — the normal-condition alignment claim versus adverse-event wealth preservation is a consequential problem that the study can plausibly investigate.
- **Plot emergence:** `partly_works` — the two tactics are meaningfully different stages of communication, but low-attention timing and press-release silence do not by themselves show that either behavior is deliberate impression management.
- **Tie–unravel alignment:** `partly_works` — the main associations are present, but moderation support is qualified for timing and the behavioral mechanism is inferred rather than observed.
- **Ending quality:** `partly_works` — the ending returns to the alignment problem but extends to stakeholder and long-run governance consequences that the design does not directly test.
- **Boundary:** This evaluates storytelling only; it is not a judgment about causal identification, the validity of the option-wealth measures, or research quality.

## Learning Affordances

### Introduction and Theory

The transferable structural move is a genuine within-construct decomposition: two components of one incentive can tell opposed stories if each has a defensible, nonredundant decision frame. This is not a license to split a predictor merely to create symmetrical hypotheses.

### Methods and Results

The card is useful for checking whether multiple outcomes represent distinct communication stages and whether claims of impression management exceed what the measures observe. It should not be used to treat calendar timing or missing disclosure as conclusive intent evidence.

### Discussion

The ending is a useful caution: a theoretical reversal from alignment to agency cost requires an observed payoff or a clearly qualified claim, not only a plausible stakeholder narrative.
