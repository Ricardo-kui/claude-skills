## Story Reading

### Theme question

How do firms try to keep stakeholder activism from materializing when a legal change makes activists harder to silence, and why is substantive institutional CSP a plausible preemptive response?

### Whole-story synopsis

The paper begins with a literature that has largely followed firms after activists have already imposed costs. Its pivot is that managers normally try to avoid such crises before they occur, yet the counterfactual risk of unmaterialized activism is hard to observe and endogenous to firm character. Anti-SLAPP enactments provide the external turn: by reducing activists' expected litigation burden, the laws make public campaigns a more credible opportunity and raise firms' prospective activism risk. The theory specifies a particular response rather than generic CSR. Firms improve institutional CSP—community, environment, and human-rights performance beyond primary exchange relationships—to build moral capital and normative legitimacy, making it harder for activists to classify them as morally culpable. This should be strongest when CSI is already covered by media, because evidence of irresponsibility makes the firm a more credible target. A firm-panel DiD then finds higher institutional CSP after enactment and a larger change for firms with more CSI coverage. Event-time and permutation exercises support the staged payoff. Supplemental analyses distinguish institutional CSP from technical CSP and CSR committees, use a reputational-risk proxy as a partial check on increased activism risk, and show a negative association between institutional CSP and NGO targeting. The discussion returns to the opening temporal reversal: activist-friendly institutions can induce firms to self-regulate before a campaign occurs, though intent and the avoided campaign remain inferred rather than observed.

### Characters and storylines

- **Threat character:** prospective stakeholder activism, an unmaterialized nonmarket risk that becomes central precisely because it is difficult to observe directly.
- **Institutional character:** anti-SLAPP law, which changes activists' capacity to make public campaigns credible by lowering the cost of retaliatory litigation.
- **Strategic character:** institutional CSP, the selected self-regulatory response intended to supply moral capital and legitimacy rather than merely structural CSR commitment.
- **Vulnerability character:** media coverage of CSI, which makes a firm easier to frame as a legitimate activist target and raises the salience of the policy-created risk.
- **Boundary characters:** technical CSP and board CSR committees, which test whether the story is about substantive institutional performance rather than a broad CSR or signaling response.
- **Storyline:** anti-SLAPP protection → higher prospective activism risk → institutional CSP as preemptive moral-capital building → reduced vulnerability to activist targeting; CSI coverage intensifies the middle link.

### Five acts

- **Exposition:** Existing work concentrates on how firms respond after stakeholder activism has become an active, costly event.
- **Rising action:** Anti-SLAPP laws make activism more feasible; firms facing an anticipatable risk can preempt it with substantive institutional CSP, especially when CSI is already visible in media.
- **Climax:** A staggered-adoption DiD shows an increase in institutional CSP and a stronger increase under high CSI media coverage.
- **Falling action:** Event-time and permutation tests stage the treatment pattern; discriminant outcomes and supplemental activism-risk/targeting analyses make the selected response more specific.
- **Denouement:** The paper reframes self-regulation as a forward-looking nonmarket strategy and positions state law as part of the multi-level opportunity structure firms face.

### Tension

- **Source:** The most consequential activism risk may be the campaign that never materializes, but its avoidance leaves neither the threat nor the counterfactual response directly visible.
- **Construction:** The paper converts this absence into a researchable tension by treating anti-SLAPP enactment as an exogenous increase in activists' opportunity and by distinguishing substantive CSP from commitment signals.

### Alternative readings

- **documented_literature_alternative:** Prior work suggests formal CSR structures can invite activism by signaling managerial receptivity. The authors distinguish that structural signal from institutional CSP as realized performance that may deter moral framing.
- **analyst_counterfactual:** The observed CSP increase may reflect a changed disclosure, stakeholder-expectations, or institutional environment rather than a purposeful attempt to deter future activism. Supplemental proxies add coherence but do not observe managerial prevention motives.

## Learning Affordances

### Introduction

The transferable move is to find the overlooked temporal position in a literature—before rather than after a threat—and explain why studying that position creates a special observation problem. It cannot be copied when the target literature is not actually reactive, or when a supposed preventive action has no credible connection to the future threat.

### Theory

The theory makes the middle consequential by naming how an observable action changes an adversary's ability to frame and target the firm, then differentiating performance from signals. It requires a genuine actor with a target-selection logic; it does not support treating every socially favorable outcome as reputational insurance.

### Methods

The design usefully places a policy shock where the otherwise latent threat enters the story. Its transfer condition is a justified jurisdictional treatment assignment and a credible source of timing variation; the method should not be used to claim observation of strategic anticipation.

### Results

The results earn their narrative role by testing the main prediction, the vulnerability boundary, timing, and discriminant measures in an ordered sequence. This works only when the alternative outcomes are theorized negative controls rather than post hoc nulls.

### Discussion

The ending demonstrates how to return from empirical outcomes to the temporal contribution. Do not inherit its claim that firms “try” to preempt a threat unless evidence goes beyond behavior consistent with that purpose.
