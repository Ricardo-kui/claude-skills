## Story Reading

### Theme question

Why do firms persist in deep, local search and constrain broad exploration when innovation requires both, and how can customer concentration shape that search through executive attention?

### Whole-story synopsis

The paper opens with the familiar importance of exploration and the observed persistence of safe, local innovation. It introduces capability and cognition accounts of myopic search, but treats their shared supply-side orientation—not a disagreement between them—as the central incompleteness. The paper then brings the demand environment into the story: dependence on a few major customers gives current customers a disproportionate place in executive attention. Existing-oriented attention becomes the bridge between an external customer structure and internal resource allocation. That bridge predicts a paired behavioral consequence: firms search more deeply in familiar knowledge while searching less broadly in unfamiliar knowledge. Performance above social aspirations and high product-market competition are introduced as forces that intensify, rather than replace, attentional capture. The empirical design first reveals the depth/breadth pattern, then tests executive attention as a partial mediator and moderators as amplifiers, with IV, DID, and alternative-measure checks providing qualified support. The Discussion returns to the opening question by naming customer concentration as a demand-side source of organizational rigidity and connecting demand structure, executive cognition, and search behavior.

### Characters and storylines

- **Main characters:** customer concentration, executive existing-oriented attention, and firm search behavior. They form the paper's environment-to-cognition-to-behavior chain.
- **Paired consequence characters:** search depth and search breadth. They show the same attentional allocation as deeper exploitation and narrower exploration.
- **Background characters:** capability and cognition views establish the supply-side baseline; they are not rival protagonists.
- **Supporting characters:** social performance feedback and product-market competition amplify the proposed attentional capture.
- **Storyline 1:** concentrated customers make the existing demand environment more salient and reinforce existing-oriented attention.
- **Storyline 2:** existing-oriented attention directs firms toward repeated use of familiar knowledge and away from novel knowledge fields.
- **Intersection:** demand-side structure enters a cognition account and produces the deep-and-narrow search pattern.

### Five acts

- **Exposition:** Innovation requires both deep and broad search, yet firms persist in local search; capability and cognition explanations establish the existing conversation.
- **Rising action:** The paper identifies their shared supply-side omission, introduces customer concentration, and develops executive existing-oriented attention as the mechanism bridge to paired search consequences.
- **Climax:** Results show that customer concentration predicts greater search depth and lower search breadth.
- **Falling action:** Mediation analyses connect concentration to attention and attention to both outcomes; performance feedback and competition show when the pattern intensifies, while IV and robustness analyses qualify the evidence.
- **Denouement:** The Discussion recasts customer concentration as a demand-side attention trap that helps explain persistent organizational myopia.

### Tension

- **Source:** A meaningful phenomenon-level tension—firms value innovation and exploration yet often search myopically—combined with the incomplete supply-side focus of existing explanations.
- **Construction:** The Introduction contrasts the importance of breadth with observed safe innovation, then shows that capability and cognition research leave demand-side stimuli outside the mechanism chain.

### Alternative readings

- **analyst_counterfactual:** A direct “customer concentration causes local search” account would identify a demand-side correlate but would not explain how customer dependence becomes an internal search allocation. This is an analyst counterfactual, not an author-stated alternative.

## Learning Affordances

### Introduction and Theory

Use Zhong to learn a mechanism bridge across literatures, not a conflict-resolution frame. The distinctive action is to show why a missing domain changes the mechanism that connects an established antecedent class to an established behavioral outcome.

### Methods and Results

The evidence story proceeds from paired behavioral outcomes to a partial attention bridge and then to amplifying conditions. It is useful only when the current study has an observable mechanism and can distinguish direct association from the mechanism claim.

### Discussion

The end point is a changed explanation of organizational myopia—demand structure becomes attention structure—not a claim that one of the prior theories lost.

## Comparison prompt

Is a new study resolving rival explanations (Zhou, Wowak, or Ridge), or is it connecting previously separate but compatible literatures through an omitted causal mechanism (Zhong)? The answer determines whether a tension-resolution story is warranted at all.
