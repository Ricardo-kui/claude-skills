# Antón et al. 2025 × DesJardine et al. 2022 — Portfolio Externalities with Conditional versus Unidirectional Payoffs

## Scope

This record compares `anton2025` and `desjardine2022`. Both make an investor's multi-firm portfolio the reason a focal firm's conduct matters beyond its standalone return. They do not make the same ownership claim.

| Card | Ownership topology | Focal action | Portfolio peer payoff | Middle mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Antón, Ederer, Giné, and Schmalz (2025) | A common owner holds the focal innovator and peer firms that may be technologically related, product-market related, or both | Focal innovation | Technological peers benefit from lower costs; product-market peers lose from business stealing | The owner internalizes two opposed cross-firm consequences, so relative proximity determines the predicted sign |
| DesJardine, Grewal, and Viswanathan (2022) | An investor owns several firms in one industry | Focal material CSR | Peer firms benefit from reduced stakeholder and regulatory spillover risk | The owner internalizes a portfolio-wide positive externality and has reason to favor a risk-reducing focal action |

## Central comparative finding

Both papers are **externality-internalization** stories, but Antón et al. make the internalized payoff inherently conditional: the same focal action can help one type of portfolio peer and harm another. DesJardine et al. instead center a single positive industry-risk pathway.

The comparison is useful only after mapping the owner, focal firm, each peer category, and the precise benefit or loss. “Common ownership” alone does not decide whether the portfolio objective implies cooperation, less competition, or a conditional mixture.

## Learning boundary for future retrieval

Retrieve `anton2025` softly for a real two-path cross-firm payoff problem, especially when a theory must explain why an average effect can cancel. Retrieve `desjardine2022` softly for a focal action that creates a portfolio-wide positive externality. Do not use either merely because a study contains common ownership, multiple firms, or a positive social outcome.

## Next comparison question

Does the proposed focal action impose one shared portfolio externality, or does it create different consequences for distinct peer relations? If the latter, what evidence establishes that the relations sort the same focal decision rather than describe different outcomes or samples?
