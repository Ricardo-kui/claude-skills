# Bala × Fang — Anticipated Category Defense versus Realized Rival Advertising

## Scope

This record compares the reviewed v0.4 cards `bala2017` and `fang2025`. Both ask how a firm not issuing the recall responds to another firm's product failure, but they do not treat the same event timing, exposure mechanism, or action problem. It is not evidence that either response generalizes across recall settings.

| Dimension | Bala et al. 2017 | Fang et al. 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Event state | A black-box warning makes a rival recall possible | A rival's recall has occurred |
| Main exposure | Category demand can shrink through physician/patient exit | Substitute evaluation can gain released demand or suffer quality comparison |
| Focal action | Pre-event category defense, then post-event sales allocation across W and B | Post-event price, quality, and brand advertising portfolio |
| Coupling mechanism | Shared sales-force scope across two categories | Differentiated advertising components and substitute similarity |
| Central implication | Promotion can preserve category viability yet substitute for later effort when resources conflict | Total advertising can conceal simultaneous preemption and comparison avoidance |

## Central comparative finding

“Competitor response to recall” should not be retrieved as a single action frame. Bala first asks whether an anticipated category-level demand loss is worth defending and then whether a multi-product competitor can exploit it without harming another product. Fang starts after a realized shock and asks how a substitute manages the opposing opportunity and comparison threat in its advertising portfolio.

```text
anticipated warning → category-defense investment → recall state → cross-category effort allocation
realized rival recall → opportunity / comparison ambiguity → decomposed advertising response → substitute sales
```

The first sequence needs a credible precursor, category-level attrition, and resource coupling. The second needs an observed event, substitutability, and action components that carry opposed comparison meanings. Neither paper licenses the generic inference that a rival recall yields more—or less—competitor promotion.

## Retrieval boundary

Retrieve Bala softly for `anticipated-rival-recall`, `category-defense-marketing`, or `cross-category-sales-scope`. Retrieve Fang softly for `mixed-opportunity-threat`, `action-decomposition`, or `horizontal-spillover`. Do not substitute one for the other merely because a project concerns a competitor's response to bad news.

## Next comparison question

When both a public warning and a realized recall are observable, does a firm use the same communication action for early category defense and later competitive capture, or do the two time points require distinct mechanisms and outcome measures?
