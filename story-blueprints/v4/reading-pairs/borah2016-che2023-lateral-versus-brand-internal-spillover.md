# Borah–Tellis × Che–Katayama–Lee — Lateral versus Brand-Internal Spillover

## Scope

This second-reading record compares `borah2016` and `che2023`. Both begin with negative information about a product and ask what happens to an uninvolved target. They are not interchangeable evidence for a generic proposition that "crises spill over."

## The two stories

| Card | Nonfocal target | Proposed bridge | Consequence that resolves the story | Boundary of the claim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Borah and Tellis (2016) | A nameplate at the same or a rival brand | Consumers encounter negative chatter and treat a similar nameplate as diagnostic of comparable quality | Negative chatter, then rival sales and stock-market loss | Perceived similarity, country of origin, and dominance shape reach and direction |
| Che, Katayama, and Lee (2023) | A known nonviolating product under Volkswagen | Consumers cannot fully isolate a product from a shared brand-level quality signal | Lower auction willingness to pay for the unaffected product | The target is inside the same brand; the event identifies the involved models clearly |

## Central comparative finding

The papers distinguish two different failures of information isolation:

1. **Lateral inference:** a consumer sees one brand as informative about a rival because the two are cognitively comparable. The central question is whether association between brands is strong enough to carry a crisis outward. (`borah2016`)
2. **Brand-internal inference:** a consumer sees an unaffected product as part of the same producer-level quality signal. The central question is why factual innocence does not block a shared-brand revaluation. (`che2023`)

The choice of evidence follows the difference. Borah and Tellis make the propagation object observable as social-media chatter and then follow it into performance. Che and colleagues make the valuation consequence observable in second-price auction bids and then inspect whether other market responses explain it. Neither paper directly observes the consumer inference, but each makes a different theoretical inference do the middle work.

## Learning boundary for future retrieval

Retrieve Borah softly for `cross-brand-negative-spillover`, `consumer-similarity-inference`, or `social-media-amplification`. Retrieve Che softly for `brand-internal-negative-spillover`, `unaffected-product-revaluation`, or `quality-signal-to-willingness-to-pay`. Do not use either as a generic template for any correlation between one firm's crisis and another firm's outcome.

## Next comparison question

When an external shock changes a focal firm's behavior rather than consumer valuation—for example, a rival recall inducing advertising or promotion—does the spillover remain an audience inference story, or does it become a strategic-response story with a different middle mechanism?
