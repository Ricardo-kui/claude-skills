# Chen 2009 × Lun 2026 × Thirumalai 2011 — Mechanism-Evidence Boundary Record

## Scope

This record compares three v0.4 cards that each use product recalls to make a theoretically rich but only partly observed mechanism consequential. It is a corpus reading aid, not a ranking of research quality and not a recommendation to use recall outcomes in an unrelated project.

| Card | Core result | Proposed mechanism | What the design actually observes | What remains inferential |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chen 2009 | Proactive recalls receive more negative abnormal returns than passive recalls. | Investors decode visible proactivity as a signal of severe hidden financial loss. | Recall strategy category, public event, returns, and observed correlates of strategy choice. | Investor beliefs, true expected losses, and whether unobserved severity selected firms into proactivity. |
| Lun 2026 | EO predicts recall incidence; COO power attenuates it, especially in late-stage portfolios. | EO strains quality safeguards; COO power makes operational discipline salient. | EO text proxy, compensation-based COO power, recalls, life-cycle portfolio proxy, and EO–QMS association. | Attention allocation, testing pace, COO intervention, QMS as a mediator, and the complete quality-control chain. |
| Thirumalai 2011 | Aggregate recall penalties are insignificant; prior recall experience predicts lower later recurrence. | Markets may expect frequent recalls; firms may learn preventively and in recall control. | Event-window returns, firm characteristics, cumulative experience, and later recall count. | Market expectations, regulatory and contracting explanations, preventive learning, control learning, and their separate effects. |

## Central comparative finding

The three cards illustrate a hierarchy of claims that must not be collapsed:

1. **Observed pattern:** an action is associated with valuation, a posture with recall incidence, or experience with lower recurrence.
2. **Proxy-consistent support:** Lun's QMS association supports one quality-management link; Chen's strategy-selection estimates and Thirumalai's heterogeneity analyses delimit their patterns.
3. **Mechanism conclusion:** investor inference, executive attention, and organizational learning remain interpretations unless their relevant beliefs, decisions, or capability changes are observed and linked.

This is not a reason to avoid mechanisms. It is a reason to state their evidentiary status with precision. Chen gains tension from audience-specific inference, Lun gains a nested governance story, and Thirumalai gains a deterrence puzzle. None should be retrieved as a model for claiming that an unobserved mechanism has been demonstrated merely because the main association is robust.

## Comparison with stronger process closure

`kalaignanam2013` is the useful contrast: it temporally places recall magnitude before reliability and reliability before later injuries and recall frequency, then reports partial mediation. Even there, root-cause search is not observed. The comparison establishes a corpus rule:

```text
association only → explanation consistent with results
association + proxy for one link → bounded process support
temporally ordered intermediate capability + mediation → stronger but still partial process closure
```

## Retrieval boundary

- Retrieve `chen2009` only when a current project has a genuinely observable external evaluation and a specified reason one visible action can have different audience meanings.
- Retrieve `lun2026` for a lower-tail consequence and nested attention boundary, not as evidence that executive compensation demonstrates operational attention.
- Retrieve `thirumalai2011` to assess whether a broad practical deterrence question is being overintegrated, or whether a learning claim lacks a capability measure.

## Next comparison question

For any proposed mechanism, which link is directly observed, which link has a proxy, and which link is only a plausible interpretation? If the answer is only the first association, should the paper present a mechanism claim, a bounded explanation, or a future research question?
