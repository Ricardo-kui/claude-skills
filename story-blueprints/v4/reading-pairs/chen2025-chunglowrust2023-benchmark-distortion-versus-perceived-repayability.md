# Short-Term Earnings, Different Middles — Benchmark-Linked Distortion versus Perceived Repayability

## Scope

This record compares `chen2025-real-earnings-management-product-recalls` and `chunglowrust2023`. Both concern present earnings benefits purchased at a later cost. They do not support a single story that “short-term pressure causes firms to cut long-term investment.”

| Card | Focal choice | Middle theoretical mechanism | Later cost | Boundary characters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chen et al. (2025) | Suspected benchmark beating through real activities management | Meeting a discrete earnings benchmark leads to production-investment cuts or overproduction that degrades product quality | Product recalls, then lower future valuation and sales growth | Career/media incentives intensify; powerful customers and growth opportunities constrain the path |
| Chung, Low, and Rust (2023) | Myopic marketing management under earnings pressure | CEO confidence makes present marketing cuts appear repayable by superior future performance | Erosion of longer-term marketing value | CMO confidence supplies counter-persuasion; board independence can intensify immediate performance pressure |

## Central comparative finding

Chen et al. require an **operational-distortion** story: the benchmark is linked to actual production choices with a quality consequence. Chung, Low, and Rust require a **perceived-repayability** story: the CEO treats the same kind of present borrowing as affordable because future performance seems sufficient to restore value. The two can share an intertemporal setting without sharing a cognitive mechanism, an operational measure, or a downstream harm.

## Comparison question

Is the proposed short-term act a change in production or resource allocation that creates a physical quality risk, or a judgment that a future loss is recoverable? Which immediate choice and later cost can the evidence actually connect?
