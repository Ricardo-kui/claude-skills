# Reading Pair — Chen, Li, and Li (2025) and Lee, Ng, Yoo, and Zhang (2026)

## Shared reading question

How should a paper position bad-news concealment when anti-SLAPP law makes external disclosure more credible: as the central construct or as the mechanism behind a later market event?

## Concrete mechanism contrast

| Reading dimension | Chen et al. — crash risk | Lee et al. — concealment of bad news |
|---|---|---|
| Primary payoff | Lower stock price crash risk: the terminal release of accumulated hidden bad news occurs less often. | Less concealment itself, triangulated by crashes, fraud probability, and firm-initiated negative press releases. |
| Middle mechanism | Ability channel changes third-party discovery/dissemination; incentive channel changes managers' expected cost of hoarding. | Public monitoring and anticipated disclosure jointly reduce managers' ability and incentive to suppress adverse information. |
| Evidence rhythm | Main crash outcome → channel boundaries → stakeholder tone → earnings/investment hoarding actions → crash rather than jump events. | Legal-premise validation → three concealment outcomes → law strength/influence and incentives → media/employee disclosure → reporting quality. |
| Narrative risk | A market-based crash proxy can be asked to stand in for too much of the upstream disclosure process. | A heterogeneous measure portfolio can blur whether all outcomes identify one construct. |
| Evidence calibration | `partly_probed`: pathway-consistent tone and manager-action proxies, but withheld news and beliefs are unobserved. | `partly_probed`: SLAPP outcomes, disclosures, and reporting measures are observed, but the complete public-stakeholder and managerial process is not. |

## Why compare them

Both papers rely on the same core institutional logic, but they place the story's climax at different points. Chen uses concealment to explain why a downstream market collapse becomes less likely. Lee makes concealment the object and uses several outcomes to triangulate it. The choice is substantive: writers should not borrow Chen's crash ending when their theory concerns disclosure behavior itself, nor borrow Lee's measurement portfolio when their contribution is the pricing consequence of accumulated bad news.

## Transfer boundary

Use this pair only when the theory specifies a meaningful ordering from outside disclosure to managerial concealment to a market outcome. Neither paper directly observes the full sequence, so the comparison does not justify treating crashes, fraud proxies, or negative press releases as interchangeable measures in every setting.
