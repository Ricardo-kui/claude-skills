# Safety Governance at Different Layers — External Voice Protection versus TMT Ideology

## Scope

This record compares `chen2026-anti-slapp-workplace-safety` and `wowak2025`. Both treat safety as shaped by governance, but they concern different governance layers, safety objects, and mechanisms.

| Card | Safety object | Governance source | Middle mechanism | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chen (2026) | Establishment workplace injuries | State anti-SLAPP statutes | Protected stakeholder voice raises exposure and accountability, prompting monitoring and workload adjustment | Injuries and workload are observed; speech, exposure, and monitoring are indirect |
| Wowak et al. (2025) | Serious medical-product recalls and response timing | TMT political ideology | Ideological values shape earlier quality priorities and later recall responsiveness through distinct processes | Two safety outcomes are separately analyzed; post hoc diversity is not a fully prepared remedy |

## Central comparative finding

Chen changes the **outside institution that makes local information costly to suppress**; Wowak changes the **decision makers whose values shape separate product-safety stages**. Workplace-injury prevention and medical-product recall count/timing should therefore not be treated as interchangeable safety outcomes, nor should legal voice protection be restated as an executive-preference mechanism.

## Comparison question

Is the proposed governance force changing who can surface safety-relevant information, how a firm monitors and allocates operational resources, or how executives prioritize quality and respond after awareness? Which safety stage does the observed outcome actually represent?
