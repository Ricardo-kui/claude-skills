# Common Ownership, Different Questions — Acquisition–Governance Tradeoff versus Portfolio Externalities

## Scope

This record compares `denicolo2025-common-ownership-governance` and `anton2025`. Both model a portfolio owner with stakes in multiple firms, but they explain different objects and require different ownership topologies.

| Card | Question to be explained | Buyer/seller and power topology | Focal consequence | Middle mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Denicolò and Panunzi (2025) | Why and how much common ownership is acquired | External diversified investor buys from each target's monitoring blockholder and, conditionally, dispersed shareholders; each blockholder retains control and monitors its own manager | Industry profit rises from softer competition while monitoring falls as blockholders sell | Equilibrium acquisition trades competition softening against weakened blockholder governance and higher reservation prices |
| Antón et al. (2025) | How a given common-ownership network affects focal innovation | Common owner holds focal innovator and distinct technology/product-market peers; no transaction or monitoring-transfer story is modeled | Peer technological benefit and peer business stealing have opposite portfolio payoffs | Portfolio objective internalizes different cross-firm externalities, so dyadic relations sort the innovation effect |

## Central comparative finding

Denicolò and Panunzi make the **ownership stake itself** the outcome and locate its cost in the transfer of residual ownership away from monitoring blockholders. Antón et al. hold an ownership network as the focal structure and ask how it changes a firm's innovation incentive through externalities received by different peers. A study cannot use the second paper's peer-externality logic to infer why stakes were acquired, or the first paper's monitoring-transfer logic without identifying the sellers and their firm-specific governance role.

## Comparison question

Does the research question concern an owner's existing portfolio-weighted incentive over a focal decision, or the transaction that created the portfolio and altered who retains monitoring power? Identify the investor, portfolio beneficiary, target firm, manager, blockholder, dispersed seller, and every peer payoff before combining the mechanisms.
