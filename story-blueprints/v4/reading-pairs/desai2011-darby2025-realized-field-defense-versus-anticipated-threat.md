# Desai × Darby — Realized Field Defense versus Anticipated Monitoring Threat

## Scope

This record compares `desai2012` and `darby2025`. Both study organizations not directly targeted by the focal event. Their nonfocal exposure, response, and audience are fundamentally different.

| Card | External event | Why the focal organization is exposed | Response | Central audience/problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Desai (2011) | Actual accidents elsewhere in the organizational field | Negative judgment may contaminate field legitimacy | Public rhetorical defense, silence, or exit | Constituents' judgment of the field's ongoing practices |
| Darby et al. (2025) | Activist attacks on other portfolio firms | Managers fear becoming a future activist target | Faster recall initiation at a non-targeted firm | Anticipated monitoring and the interpretation of proactive operational action |

## Central comparative finding

Desai is a **realized legitimacy-contagion** story. The organization acts only when silence will not prevent fieldwide judgment or when its defense remains credible; the response is primarily rhetorical and field-preserving.

Darby is an **anticipated sanction** story. The organization is not yet under the negative judgment at issue; it changes a post-awareness operational response to reduce its chance of future targeting. Its action is not collective field defense.

## Learning boundary for future retrieval

Retrieve Desai softly for `field-legitimacy-defense`, `indirect-crisis-exposure`, or `collective-rhetorical-response`; retrieve Darby for `anticipated-monitoring`, `spillover-effect`, or `proactive-response-to-threat`. Do not infer a shared mechanism from the fact that both focal organizations were not the original target of the event.

## Next comparison question

When a firm publicly responds after a rival's crisis, is it trying to protect field legitimacy, distinguish itself from the rival, or signal preventive competence to a future monitor? Those responses need separate audience and credibility accounts.
