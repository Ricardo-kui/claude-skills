# DesJardine et al. 2023 × DesJardine et al. 2025 — Media versus Rating-Agency Intermediary Competition

## Scope

This record compares `desjardine2023` and `desjardine2025`. Both study an investor who owns an information intermediary and firms that rival a target. Neither is a general finding that any common owner captures an information channel.

| Card | Intermediary | Ownership topology | Competitive output | Middle mechanism | Evidence limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DesJardine, Shi, and Cheng (2023) | Media outlet | Investor → media company and target's rivals | More negative coverage of target | Owner influence plus media-executive catering changes the information environment | No observed instruction or editorial filtering |
| DesJardine, Li, and Shi (2025) | ESG rating agency | Investor → MSCI and target's rivals | Lower ESG rating for target | Owner influence or rater catering enables a rating disadvantage under reputational windows | No observed investor-to-rater influence or rating decision process |

## Central comparative finding

The cards share a **third-party information-channel** logic, but their story engines differ. The 2023 paper makes a general invisible-hand argument: direct coordination is conspicuous, so common owners may influence a media channel whose senior executives have their own shareholder-facing incentives. Its main conditions diagnose owner motivation, owner power, and intermediary receptivity.

The 2025 paper makes a narrower information-based-competition argument: the rating agency's comparative ESG assessment can be shifted against a target, particularly as reputational threats make an attack valuable and reputational opportunities make it more feasible. Its threat and opportunity windows are not substitutes for the 2023 paper's bilateral influence chain.

## Learning boundary for future retrieval

Retrieve either card softly for `information-intermediary-bias`, `indirect-competitive-attack`, or `ownership-network-topology`. Use `desjardine2023` when learning how to establish the actor–channel–tie–target topology and bilateral incentives. Use `desjardine2025` when learning how a reputational information outcome may have changing motivational and receptivity windows. In either case, name the intermediary's actual discretion and do not claim direct capture from ownership ties alone.

## Next comparison question

When a common owner affects an information environment, is the intermediary selecting public coverage, assigning an evaluative score, or merely transmitting information it cannot meaningfully alter? The answer determines whether the invisible-hand comparison is structurally available.
