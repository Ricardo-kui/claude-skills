# Advertising Around a Recall — Prerecall Investor Signal versus Postrecall Demand Repair

## Scope

This record compares `gao2015-prerecall-advertising-stock-value` and `liu2015`. Both study advertising around automobile recalls, but their advertising actions occur at different temporal points and solve different audience problems.

| Card | Timing and audience | Focal advertising role | Middle mechanism | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gao et al. (2015) | Before the recall announcement; investors | A prerecall spending deviation is later reinterpreted once hidden defect information becomes public | Advertising signals private confidence but can create quality expectations whose violation intensifies disappointment | Announcement-window abnormal stock return |
| Liu and Shankar (2015) | During and after repeated recalls; consumers | Advertising is a repair lever whose effectiveness may itself be weakened by the crisis | Recall information directly damages persistent preference and indirectly lowers advertising effectiveness, with parent-brand spillovers | Dynamic nameplate demand and inferred brand preference |

## Central comparative finding

Gao et al. ask how investors retrospectively interpret an action that preceded disclosure of a hidden crisis. Liu and Shankar ask how customers respond after a known crisis changes the preference state and the effectiveness of repair communication. One paper's advertising variable is a **signal about hidden severity**; the other's is an **intervention inside the damage process**.

## Comparison question

At what point relative to awareness does advertising act, who interprets it, and is the claim about private information, customer repair, or both? Which observed outcome belongs to that audience and temporal position?
