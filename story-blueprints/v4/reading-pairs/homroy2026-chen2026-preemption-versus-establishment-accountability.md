# Reading Pair — Homroy (2026) and Chen (2026)

## Shared reading question

How can the same legal protection for stakeholder speech lead to different firm responses without reducing anti-SLAPP statutes to a generic governance treatment?

## Concrete mechanism contrast

| Reading dimension | Homroy — free speech and innovation | Chen — speech and workplace safety |
|---|---|---|
| Information at stake | Civil-society criticism makes the social cost of an incumbent technology publicly credible. | Employees and local observers can surface unsafe conditions, workload pressure, and hazards. |
| Focal organizational response | Firms with absorptive capacity preempt an anticipated industry transition by patenting cleaner or novel alternatives. | Establishments anticipate greater accountability and respond through closer headquarters oversight and lower workload pressure. |
| Strategic horizon | Intertemporal and competitive: secure patent rents before rivals adapt. | Operational and local: prevent near-term injuries before hazards become consequential. |
| Boundary prediction | Effects concentrate in high-capacity, reputation-exposed, and less financially constrained firms. | Effects concentrate where retention and local reputational costs make hazardous exposure more costly, and where headquarters monitoring is easier. |
| Evidence calibration | `partly_probed`: policy, innovation type, heterogeneity, litigation, and reputation probes are observed; strategic anticipation and criticism are not. | `partly_probed`: injury outcomes, statutory strength, proximity, hours, and staffing are observed; voice, exposure, and audit activity are not. |

## Why compare them

Both papers use anti-SLAPP adoption to move from protected expression to accountability, but they assign the middle of the story to different organizational problems. Homroy asks how firms reposition technology portfolios when an incumbent becomes reputationally fragile. Chen asks how local establishments reduce immediately observable harm when employee and community voice becomes harder to dismiss. The comparison prevents a writer from borrowing the policy shock while blurring whether the relevant response is strategic preemption, internal monitoring, workload adjustment, financing, or something else.

## Transfer boundary

Use this pair only when a legal or institutional change genuinely changes the cost or credibility of stakeholder voice. It does not establish that every transparency reform produces either innovation or safety, nor that either paper directly observes the voice-to-response sequence.
