# Li × Fang — Vertical and Horizontal Recall Spillover

## Scope

This record compares the reviewed v0.4 cards `li2026` and `fang2025`. It separates two relationship structures through which one product recall can affect other firms. It is not evidence that every recall creates both effects or that supplier value loss and substitute sales are comparable outcome measures.

| Dimension | Li 2026 | Fang 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Exposed actor | Supplier tied to the recalling buyer | Substitute brand in the same product category |
| Relationship basis | Buyer–supplier interdependence and supply-network position | Consumer substitutability and perceived similarity |
| Main spillover outcome | Supplier firm-value loss | Substitute sales lift and advertising adjustment |
| Transmission logic | Cash-flow disruption plus guilt-by-association evaluation | Competition for released demand plus contagion through comparison |
| Central learning move | Tie as both pipe and prism | Aggregate advertising decomposed into opposed action components |

## Central comparative finding

The term **spillover** is not a sufficient retrieval category. The relationship that exposes the observer determines the plausible story:

```text
buyer recall → supplier tie → expected disruption / linked evaluation → value loss
buyer recall → substitute comparison → competition / contagion ambiguity → response portfolio and sales
```

Li's supplier need not compete with the buyer's product and can be harmed despite operational innocence. Fang's substitute can gain sales despite fearing contamination, and its advertising response is a strategic attempt to manage that ambiguity. Therefore, neither a shared industry label nor a simple “related firm” category can replace a stated exposure mechanism.

## Retrieval boundary

Retrieve Li softly for `vertical-spillover`, `supply-chain-risk-propagation`, or `indirect-information-intermediaries`. Retrieve Fang softly for `horizontal-spillover`, `mixed-opportunity-threat`, or `action-decomposition`. Do not recommend one merely because a project says “recall spillover.”

## Next comparison question

The next targeted re-reading could find a paper where one organization is simultaneously a supplier and a competitor, so the corpus can test whether operational exposure and consumer comparison can genuinely coexist or must be modeled as separate stories.
