# Li × Kalaignanam — Quality Risk and Learning Sequence

## Scope

This record compares the reviewed v0.4 cards `li2026geography` and `kalaignanam2013`. It is a corpus reading aid, not a claim that their measures can be combined in one empirical model or a recommendation that every quality-risk study needs a learning-after-failure storyline.

## Central distinction

| Card | Theoretical object | Temporal location | Outcome meaning | Key boundary lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Li 2026 | Geographic architecture of the direct supply base | Before or during quality production | Consumer complaints as system-level quality risk | Distance and dispersion are different constructs; institutional quality and certification alter their consequences. |
| Kalaignanam 2013 | Organizational learning induced by recall magnitude | After a realized recall | Future reliability, injuries, and recall frequency | A recall can improve later safety only when failure prompts transferable and attended learning. |

Li explains a risk-producing context; Kalaignanam explains a possible response and subsequent improvement after a failure. The comparison therefore forms a **sequence of questions**, not a unified causal result:

```text
spatial supply-base architecture → system-level quality risk → recall event → reliability learning → later safety outcomes
```

Neither paper establishes every arrow. Li does not show that its complaints become recalls or identify supplier-caused defects. Kalaignanam does not show that supply-base geography causes recall magnitude or that observed reliability change is a direct record of learning routines.

## Comparative learning finding

The pair guards against three collapsed claims:

1. A lower recall count can mean fewer quality failures, fewer disclosures, or successful learning after a prior recall; its meaning depends on the paper's temporal position and process evidence.
2. Product quality risk and product reliability are related but not interchangeable: Li measures realized complaints, whereas Kalaignanam uses a make-level reliability score and downstream injuries/recalls.
3. A structural condition that generates risk is not automatically a condition that prevents learning. Li's dispersion story is about coordination before failure; Kalaignanam's shared-assets story is about transfer after failure.

## Retrieval boundary

Retrieve Li softly for `construct-disaggregation`, `coordination-versus-monitoring`, or `contextual-boundary`. Retrieve Kalaignanam softly for `learning-from-failure`, `reliability-mediated-safety-outcome`, or `transfer-of-learning`. Do not use a match on the word “quality” to recommend either card without a stated question about the relevant temporal process.
