# Mukherjee and Sinha 2018 × Thirumalai and Sinha 2011 — Decision Calibration versus Recall Consequences

## Scope

This record compares `mukherjee2018` and `thirumalai2011`. Both study medical-device recalls, but their theoretical objects occupy different points in the recall sequence.

| Card | Temporal location | Focal actor and outcome | Middle mechanism | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mukherjee and Sinha (2018) | Before or at the recall decision | Manufacturer/regulator; inferred false-alarm versus miss bias from adverse-event signals | Signal detectability and distributed attention change the recall threshold and error direction | The model infers bias from predicted recall need; it does not observe thresholds or cognition |
| Thirumalai and Sinha (2011) | After a recall has occurred | Investors and firms; announcement returns and later recall recurrence | Expected deterrence, recovery capacity, operational focus, and inferred learning | It observes returns and recurrence, not market expectations or organizational learning routines |

## Central comparative finding

Mukherjee and Sinha explain whether a firm is calibrated to turn adverse-event feedback into a recall decision. Thirumalai and Sinha explain what a realized recall implies for market valuation and future recurrence. Under-reaction in the first paper must not be relabeled as the later recall-count process in the second, and lower recurrence in the second does not reveal calibrated initial detection.

## Learning boundary for future retrieval

Retrieve `mukherjee2018` softly for a genuine threshold problem with two error types and a defensible reference for calibration. Retrieve `thirumalai2011` for an anomaly that connects realized recall consequences and recurrence while preserving their distinct evidence arenas. A common industry, outcome label, or FDA data source does not make the mechanisms interchangeable.
