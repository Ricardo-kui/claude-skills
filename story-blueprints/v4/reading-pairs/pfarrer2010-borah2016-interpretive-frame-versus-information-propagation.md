# Collective Perception Around Adverse Events — Interpretive Frame versus Information Propagation

## Scope

This record compares `pfarrer2010-reputation-celebrity-earnings-surprises` and `borah2016`. Both explain why collective perceptions matter when an adverse or unexpected event occurs. They do not offer one general mechanism called “reputation affects event consequences.”

| Card | Focal event and target | Middle mechanism | What perception does | Key boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pfarrer et al. (2010) | A firm's own positive or negative earnings surprise, read by its investors | Reputation supplies an analytical ability frame; celebrity supplies an affective potential frame | Changes how an audience interprets an event at the focal firm | Positive versus negative surprise; the two frames do not yield identical predictions in both valences |
| Borah and Tellis (2016) | One automobile nameplate's recall, with a rival nameplate as target | Recall-triggered chatter becomes accessible and diagnostic through perceived similarity | Carries concern from a focal brand to an otherwise uninvolved brand | Country-of-origin similarity and dominance govern reach and direction |

## Central comparative finding

Pfarrer et al. ask how an audience **reads its own firm's surprise** through a pre-existing collective frame. Borah and Tellis ask how an audience **moves concern from one firm's event to another firm** through a transmission process. The former requires a theory of the focal audience's expectation and information processing; the latter requires a theory of why the source event is diagnostic of the nonfocal target.

## Comparison question

Does the proposed construct alter the interpretation of evidence about the focal organization, or does it make evidence about one organization travel to another? What source, receiving target, and inferential bridge would need to be observed for a spillover claim?
