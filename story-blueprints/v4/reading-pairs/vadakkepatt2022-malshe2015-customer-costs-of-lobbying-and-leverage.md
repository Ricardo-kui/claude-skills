# Vadakkepatt et al. × Malshe and Agarwal — Customer Costs of Lobbying and Leverage

## Scope

This record compares `vadakkepatt2022` and `malshe2015`. Both challenge a one-sided firm-value account by tracing a strategic choice into customer satisfaction. They are not evidence for a generic claim that all nonmarketing investments hurt customers.

| Card | Focal choice | Direct firm-level benefit | Customer-cost mechanism | How customer satisfaction returns to value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vadakkepatt et al. (2022) | Lobbying | Regulatory capture and political advantage | Limited attention shifts from customer priorities to regulators | Satisfaction is a negative indirect path that counteracts lobbying's direct value benefit |
| Malshe and Agarwal (2015) | Financial leverage | Average capital-structure benefit and debt discipline | Financial pressure constrains advertising, lowering satisfaction | Leverage weakens the value investors attach to satisfaction's future growth options |

## Central comparative finding

Both papers use customer satisfaction to change the meaning of a strategy that can look financially attractive. But their middle stories are distinct.

1. **Attention diversion:** Lobbying directs the firm's attention toward a different stakeholder arena. Customer-facing priorities can be aligned or counterbalanced without eliminating lobbying. (`vadakkepatt2022`)
2. **Resource constraint and valuation:** Leverage tightens financial flexibility, with advertising as the supported route to lower satisfaction; it also changes the market value of a satisfied customer base. (`malshe2015`)

Thus, one study is about what the organization attends to, while the other is about what financial commitments leave it able to fund and monetize. The shared outcome does not make their mechanisms substitutes.

## Learning boundary for future retrieval

Retrieve `vadakkepatt2022` softly for `nonmarket-strategy-customer-cost`, `attention-reallocation`, or `competing-value-paths`. Retrieve `malshe2015` for `capital-structure-customer-consequence`, `financial-constraint-on-marketing`, or `customer-satisfaction-valuation-boundary`. Before using either, specify whether the proposed problem is attentional reallocation, resource constraint, or both—and do not call a correlated drop in satisfaction evidence of either mechanism by itself.

## Next comparison question

When firms pursue political influence under financial pressure, do attention diversion and resource constraint compound, offset, or operate in different phases of the customer-value process? A future comparison should only combine these stories where that interaction is theorized rather than inferred from shared outcomes.
