# Executive Incentives Across Product-Safety Decision Stages — Second Reading Record

## Scope

This record compares four v0.4 cards that connect CEO compensation or wealth exposure to product-safety outcomes. It is a corpus boundary, not a claim that all four papers estimate one common mechanism or that a project with executive incentives should reproduce this sequence.

| Card | Theoretical object | Decision/process stage | Mechanism evidence check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wowak 2015 | Option-pay share and lack of caution | Upstream product design, production, and quality-control process, represented by serious recall incidence | `not_directly_tested` — the proposed cognition and quality-control chain is not measured. |
| Darby 2024 | CEO stock ownership and private exposure to bad-news costs | Known-defect awareness to recall initiation, followed by announcement-period market response | `partly_probed` — the decision and later penalty are temporally observed, but the CEO-level route to delay is not. |
| Malik 2025 | Current versus prospective option wealth | Public handling once a medical-device recall is being initiated or announced | `partly_probed` — tactics and media conditions are observed; impression-management intent is inferred. |
| Liu 2016 | Cash pay, equity incentive, and tenure as conditions on decision weights | Post-recall full versus partial consumer remedy | `partly_probed` — the choice and criterion interactions are observed; CEO reweighting and consumer restoration are not. |

## Central comparative finding

The same label—"CEO incentives and recalls"—would conceal four distinct narrative objects:

```text
incentive condition
  ├─ upstream safety production / failure incidence → Wowak 2015
  ├─ known-defect decision timing → Darby 2024
  ├─ public visibility and communication tactics → Malik 2025
  └─ consumer remedy after recall → Liu 2016
```

The measures are therefore not interchangeable operationalizations. Wowak's binary and count outcomes are two measures of one incidence construct; Darby, Malik, and Liu address separate later decision margins. A writer may learn from a structurally analogous incentive reversal across these cards, but must first specify which process stage their own outcome represents.

## Mechanism-evidence boundary

The theoretical mechanisms remain the middle of each paper's story. The short labels above merely calibrate what the empirical evidence can claim. A precise decision-stage outcome can test an incentive association or interaction without by itself demonstrating intervening attention, motive, or organizational practice.

## Retrieval boundary

- Retrieve `wowak2015` for an upstream incidence story only when the outcome genuinely represents production, prevention, or safety-failure occurrence—not a post-event response.
- Retrieve `darby2024` for a consequential timing decision, not for generic recall frequency.
- Retrieve `malik2025` for differentiated public-response tactics, not for the duration of operational investigation.
- Retrieve `liu2016` when an observed response choice contains two named criteria whose weights an executive condition can plausibly change.

## Next comparison question

If a study uses a recall outcome, which clock and decision margin does it actually capture? What observation would be required before the paper may move from "incentive-associated pattern" to a claim about CEO attention, organizational safety practice, or stakeholder-directed intent?
