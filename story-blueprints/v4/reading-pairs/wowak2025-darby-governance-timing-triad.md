# Wowak × Darby Governance-and-Timing Triad — Second Reading Record

## Scope

This is a comparison of four v0.4 cards: the seed `wowak2025` and the Darby set `darby2024`, `darby2025`, and `darby2026`. It is a corpus learning record, not a project-story recommendation and not evidence that any governance study should use recall timing.

All four papers use the medical-product setting and treat `defect awareness → recall initiation` as a meaningful decision window. That common measurement window makes the comparison unusually informative, but it does not make their theoretical stories interchangeable.

## What the Darby set adds to the Wowak seed

| Card | Governing force | Story question | How timing functions | Main caution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wowak 2025 | TMT political ideology | Why can one higher-order safety theme yield fewer serious recalls but slower response? | One of two distinct safety processes, paired with recall count | Do not split dimensions unless they are genuinely different processes. |
| Darby 2023/2024 | CEO stock ownership | Can a familiar alignment device induce a delay that later harms the value it seemed to protect? | One discretionary decision whose later consequence reverses its apparent rationale | Three mechanisms make one prediction, but are not separately identified. |
| Darby 2025 | Activists that attacked other firms | Can a visible threat elsewhere make untargeted firms act preemptively? | A signal of proactive response to anticipated future monitoring | Fast action is inferred as impression management, not observed as such. |
| Darby 2026 | Large institutional owners | Can resourceful outside owners indirectly monitor a faster response, and when does opacity weaken that influence? | An operational response shaped by routine external monitoring | The external-governance gap is credible but not a deep theoretical conflict. |

## Central comparative finding

The triad does **not** establish the general rule “governance makes recalls faster or slower.” It instead separates three questions that would be blurred by such a rule:

1. **Incentive exposure:** Does the decision maker internalize an immediate loss and therefore risk delay? (`darby2024`)
2. **Anticipated sanction:** Does a visible attack on another actor induce preemptive action without direct treatment? (`darby2025`)
3. **Monitoring capacity:** Can a powerful external owner influence the decision, and what opacity limits that influence? (`darby2026`)

Wowak is the necessary contrast. Its political-ideology result is not another version of these governance antecedent studies: recall count concerns an earlier quality-production process, whereas time-to-recall concerns the later response once a defect is known. The Darby papers hold attention on the latter process and change the story by changing the governing force.

## Learning boundaries for future retrieval

The Darby cards may be softly retrieved for signals such as `self-defeating-incentive`, `decision-consequence-chain`, `anticipated-monitoring`, `spillover-effect`, `external-monitoring`, or `information-asymmetry-boundary`. A signal only asks whether a current writing problem may be structurally comparable.

It does not authorize any of the following transfers:

- treating a project as a recall-timing study because it has a delayed outcome;
- framing any outside audience as a monitor without an observable pathway of attention or leverage;
- claiming a self-defeating decision where the later consequence was not part of the original theoretical promise;
- treating multiple outcome variables as distinct processes without an independently defensible process distinction.

## Next comparison question

The next seed-led search should not look for another governance antecedent mechanically. It should find a paper where the timing decision's **information-production stage** is itself central (for example, investigation effort, verification, or endogenous awareness), so the corpus can test whether the Darby cards over-locate discretion in the final initiation decision.
