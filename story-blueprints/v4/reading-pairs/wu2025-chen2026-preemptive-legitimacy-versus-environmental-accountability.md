# Reading Pair — Wu, Bruton, and Krause (2025) and Chen, Ke, and Kuang (2026)

## Shared reading question

When anti-SLAPP law makes stakeholder criticism harder to suppress, what exactly is the firm trying to prevent—and what type of response makes that prevention credible?

## Concrete mechanism contrast

| Reading dimension | Wu et al. — activism risk and institutional CSP | Chen et al. — speech protection and toxic emissions |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate threat | Becoming an attractive target for a future stakeholder-activist campaign. | Public exposure of concrete environmental negligence and its reputational consequences. |
| Core response | Institutional CSP builds moral capital and normative legitimacy that complicate activist framing. | Environmental investment and governance reduce toxic releases and institutionalize sustainability decisions. |
| Claimed prevention | Deter a would-be campaign before it materializes. | Prevent or reduce an operational harm before or as scrutiny becomes more consequential. |
| Central boundary | CSI media coverage makes a firm more morally contestable and raises activism vulnerability. | NGO/government-client pressure and sustainability-aligned incentives make environmental scrutiny more consequential or actionable. |
| Evidence calibration | `partly_probed`: law, CSP, CSI coverage, risk proxy, and a supplemental CSP–targeting association are observed; intended preemption and counterfactual activism are not. | `partly_probed`: law, releases, environmental news, investment/governance practices, and stable activity are observed; speech, reputation, and intermediary sequencing are not. |

## Why compare them

Both papers reject a generic “speech protection improves CSR” reading. Wu's middle is anticipatory legitimacy management: actual institutional CSP is valuable because it makes a firm harder to portray as morally culpable. Chen's middle is more operational: reduced toxic releases become credible through investments, governance systems, and a no-contraction test. The pair helps a writer decide whether the theory concerns an adversary's targeting calculus, the prevention of a particular harm, or both—and prevents substituting one story for the other merely because the legal shock is shared.

## Transfer boundary

Use this comparison only where stakeholder voice is plausibly consequential and the response can be tied either to targetability or to a particular harm. Neither paper directly observes the full voice-to-reputation-to-response sequence, so the pair cannot support claims that every legal speech protection automatically improves corporate social performance.
