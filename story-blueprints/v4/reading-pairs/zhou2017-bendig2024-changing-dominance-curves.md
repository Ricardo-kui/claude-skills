# Changing-Dominance Curves — Zhou 2017 × Bendig 2024

## Scope

Both cards derive a nonlinear result from mechanisms whose relative importance changes, but they are not interchangeable “inverted-U exemplars.” This record distinguishes their theoretical jobs and prevents the curve from becoming the retrieval criterion.

| Card | Focal outcome | What changes dominance | Curve's theoretical meaning | Mechanism evidence check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zhou 2017 | Innovation performance | State ownership improves allocation of R&D inputs while eventually weakening utilization of those inputs | A positive outcome peaks when allocation benefit and utilization cost are jointly balanced | `partly_probed` in its own card's evidence architecture. |
| Bendig 2024 | Serious product-recall likelihood | Immediate search, coordination, and disruption costs are later countered by venturing experience and learning routines | An adverse outcome peaks when early costs have accumulated but later learning benefits are not yet dominant | `not_directly_tested` — the curve is observed, not the proposed cost-learning transition. |

## Central comparative finding

An inverted U does not itself identify a story. First identify the outcome's direction and the schedule that could change the net effect:

```text
positive outcome: benefit first → cost later → Zhou 2017
adverse outcome: cost first → learning benefit later → Bendig 2024
```

Zhou integrates two facets of one innovation process. Bendig uses a shared cost-learning trunk for two external-venturing modes and then makes their governance difference consequential under turbulence. In neither case may a writer infer a curve merely from the existence of two mechanisms.

## Retrieval boundary

- Retrieve `zhou2017` only for a real theoretical conflict or facet integration in the same focal process.
- Retrieve `bendig2024` when activity accumulation has a justified early burden and a later capability-building mechanism, and when the outcome is an adverse one whose peak has substantive meaning.
