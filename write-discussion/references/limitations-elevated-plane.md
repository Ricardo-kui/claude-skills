# Limitations, Future Research, and Elevated Plane

Use this reference for limitations structure, future research directions, and conclusion elevation.

## Limitations Standard Structure

> "The findings for... must be interpreted with some caution. First, [limitation 1 and why it matters for interpretation]. Second, [limitation 2 and why it matters]. Third, [limitation 3]."

**Key principles**:
- Explain constraints on interpretation: limitations are not just "future research should examine..." but explain how this limitation affects current conclusion interpretation
- Do not outsource reviewer worries to future research: proactively discuss the threat level of limitations to interpretation
- Convert to future research: each limitation can be converted into a specific future research question

## Future Research Direction Template

> "Future research could extend our findings by [specific suggestion]. For example, [elaboration]. Another promising direction would be to [suggestion]."

## Elevated Plane (Conclusion Elevation)

**Standard template**:
> "In conclusion, this study demonstrates that [core finding]. By [method/theoretical lens], we have shown that [contribution]. These findings suggest that the conversation on [topic] has moved from [old position] to [new position]. [Final insight with concrete implication]."

**Key characteristics**:
- Return to Introduction opening (show conversation has changed)
- One-sentence summary of core contribution
- Stop at **elevated plane** (a more abstract, more universal insight)
- Final sentence must be specific, not a generic "more research is needed"

**Elevated plane example**:
> "Ultimately, our findings suggest that the very behaviors managers use to build their own social capital may inadvertently erode the social capital of those they seek to influence—a paradox that deserves greater attention in future research on organizational relationships."
> — Source: Keeves 2017
