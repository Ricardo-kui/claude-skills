# Incommensurability introduction routing

Read this file only when the primary gap is Incommensurability. Use the route already diagnosed by `research-gap-diagnosis`; if absent, run the authenticity gate and four-route decision sequence before drafting.

## Abstraction ladder: precision without overfitting

Use four levels. Finer classification should improve retrieval and comparison, not create a mandatory template for every subtype.

| Level | What to preserve | Permitted effect on the skill | Overfitting boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L0 stable narrative kernel** | Fairly build the strongest prior accounts; identify the exact incompatibility; explain why it matters; preview an adjudicating resolution; preserve what remains valid in prior theory | May constrain every Incommensurability Introduction after cross-paper validation | Do not encode paragraph count, stock phrasing, or a specific empirical setting |
| **L1 resolution family** | R1 X differentiation, R2 Y disaggregation, R3 opposing mechanisms, or R4 contextual contingency | Select required reasoning functions and relevant corpus candidates | Route does not determine exact module order, Hook, paragraph count, or theory vocabulary |
| **L2 narrative tactic** | How a paper stages the route: consensus→anomaly, two-stream debate, theory→reality contradiction, audience split, delayed resolution reveal, and similar moves | Store as an optional, evidence-tagged corpus variant | Never promote from one paper; a new label requires a different persuasive action, not different wording |
| **L3 paper signature** | Named cases, exact metaphors, paragraph count, journal-specific flourishes, empirical details, and memorable sentences | Keep as an evidence anchor or exemplar note only | Never convert directly into routing or a reusable skeleton |

When route confidence is low, preserve L0, report the two most plausible L1 routes, and postpone L2 selection. Do not force a paper into R1–R4 merely to complete metadata; retain an `unclassified_residual` when the full text reveals another conflict location.

### Stable narrative kernel

Across R1–R4, extract these invariant functions before studying surface form:

1. **Common ground**: what strong prior claim(s) does the paper ask the reader to accept?
2. **Exact incompatibility**: which predictions, observations, or theoretical premises cannot jointly hold as currently stated?
3. **Diagnostic location**: where does the paper locate the problem—X, Y, causal process, or context?
4. **Resolution operator**: what conceptual operation transforms the contradiction into adjudicable predictions?
5. **Reader conversion**: what should the reader believe before versus after the Introduction?
6. **Contribution conversion**: how does resolving the conflict change theory rather than merely organize findings?

## Two-stage comparability gate

### Stage A — what the Introduction must establish

The literature conversation needs a **shared theoretical object**, not identical low-level indicators. Accept comparability when rival claims address:

- the same umbrella construct or strategic phenomenon;
- members of a defensible higher-order X or Y family; and
- the same consequential theoretical question at that higher level.

The Introduction must explicitly map the lower-order constructs/outcomes into that shared object. “Both concern firm outcomes” is too broad; “both evaluate the consequences of external venturing for the parent firm and stakeholders” is a defensible mapping when the included outcomes genuinely serve that judgment.

Do not reject R1/R2 because prior studies use different concrete X or Y members—the differentiation is the resolution. Reject only when the family is post hoc, normatively convenient, or unable to support a common theoretical claim.

### Stage B — what the Introduction previews for Theory

When the selected route is R3 or R4, the Introduction should name the intended focal relation, but the strict lock occurs when Theory begins formal hypothesis derivation. At that point the paper must hold constant the concrete X, Y, unit/level, time horizon, and estimand; only mechanism dominance (R3) or W state (R4) may change.

For R1/R2, preview the umbrella construct/family and the differentiated members instead of pretending the concrete X/Y were identical all along.

## Front-end contract

The opening functional units should establish the conflict before the Introduction commits to a resolution. They usually occur within the first three units, but may be merged, reordered, or distributed across paragraphs:

1. **Consequential puzzle**: show the real-world pattern or decision for which opposite predictions matter; a literature consensus or mature theoretical debate may carry this function when its practical consequence is made explicit early.
2. **Strongest prior conversation**: present both sides in their strongest form, including why each is reasonable and what each predicts.
3. **Conflict diagnosis**: locate the hidden aggregation, competing process, or omitted condition and state why the current literature cannot adjudicate the conflict.

Do not open with “findings are mixed” and immediately announce a moderator. The reader must first see the shared theoretical object or defensible family, the genuinely incompatible claims at that level, and the theoretical trouble they create.

## Route-specific dialogue moves

| Route | P2 literature dialogue | P3 diagnostic turn | Theory-lens preview | Contribution promise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1 X-side differentiation** | Show how both camps use the same umbrella X while relying on different implied features | Identify the unitary-X assumption and define the theoretically prior X types | “We distinguish X₁ from X₂ and argue that each activates a different process toward Y.” | Replace a unitary predictor with a type-sensitive explanation and predictions |
| **R2 Y-side disaggregation** | Show how studies speak of Y as one outcome despite examining different functions, audiences, levels, or horizons | Identify the unitary-Y assumption and explain what aggregation conceals | “We disaggregate Y into Y₁ and Y₂ and explain why X has different consequences for each.” | Replace an omnibus outcome claim with outcome-specific theory |
| **R3 opposing mechanisms** | Build the positive and negative accounts symmetrically | Show that neither account specifies relative dominance across X, time, or states | “We identify two countervailing mechanisms and theorize how their relative strength produces [adjudicating pattern].” | Reconcile the conflict through a dominance schedule or discriminating test |
| **R4 contextual contingency** | Show that both accounts may be valid but silently assume different W states | Name the omitted boundary and explain which mechanism W changes | “We argue that W determines whether mechanism A or B dominates, producing directly distinguishable conditional predictions.” | Convert an unconditional dispute into conditional theory |

## Sentence skeletons

Use these as logic carriers, not as copy-ready prose:

- **R1**: “Prior research has treated [X] as a unitary construct. Yet the arguments supporting opposite predictions rely on different forms of [X]: [X₁] and [X₂]. Distinguishing them changes what [X] should do to [Y].”
- **R2**: “The apparent disagreement rests on an omnibus conception of [Y]. Once [Y₁] is separated from [Y₂], the same [X] need not have a common consequence across both outcomes.”
- **R3**: “Each account captures a real process, but neither specifies how the two processes combine. We theorize their relative dominance and derive [a sign change / threshold / curvilinear pattern / adjudicating prediction].”
- **R4**: “The competing predictions are conditional rather than universally incompatible. [W] changes [specific mechanism], such that [X] produces [prediction A] under [W state 1] but [prediction B] under [W state 2].” Use a sign reversal when theory warrants it; use effect-versus-null only when the null is deductively derived and directly tested.

## Safeguards

- Do not equate different operationalizations with different X or Y types unless the theoretical object itself differs.
- Do not require identical concrete Y indicators at the conversation gate when they are defensible members of one higher-order outcome family.
- Do not use a vague umbrella such as “firm outcomes” to force comparability; name the shared theoretical judgment and map every member into it.
- For R3/R4, do not carry conversation-level flexibility into formal hypotheses: lock concrete X, Y, unit/level, horizon, and estimand before deriving rival or conditional predictions.
- Do not promise U/倒U solely from “two opposing mechanisms”; preview a curve only after specifying how mechanism strength changes along X.
- Do not label a same-sign magnitude moderator as Incommensurability. Use Inadequacy × Boundary unless W explains a real reversal or another deductively incompatible state. A significant effect in one subgroup and a nonsignificant effect in another is not such a state; an effect-versus-null claim qualifies only when the null is theoretically derived and the conditional difference is tested directly.
- Keep one primary resolution route and at most one secondary route. More than two usually scatters the contribution.
- Preserve continuity: the route named in P3, the theory-lens paragraph, the preview, the contribution paragraph, and `paper-state.yaml` must match.
- Treat every route-specific sequence in the routing table as illustrative. Select module order from the paper's actual story contract and conversation strategy.

### Anti-overfitting tests

Before creating or promoting a pattern, ask:

1. **Leave-one-paper-out**: can the pattern explain another full-text paper without retaining the source paper's setting or wording?
2. **Minimal-sufficient abstraction**: if a detail is removed, does the persuasive action remain? If yes, keep the detail at L3.
3. **Counterexample tolerance**: could a strong Introduction violate this tactic while preserving L0? If yes, it cannot be a universal rule.
4. **Route stability**: does the R1–R4 diagnosis remain the same when the contribution label or journal style changes? If not, report low confidence.
5. **Functional novelty**: does a proposed subtype change the persuasive action or resolution operator? Different wording, paragraph count, industry, or theory name is not a new subtype.

## Evolution hook

When a distilled full-text exemplar uses Incommensurability, tag its primary/secondary R1–R4 route and any failure of this taxonomy. Do not force-fit a paper: a recurring hybrid or counterexample belongs in the skill-design feedback loop before changing core routing.
