# Process transition operators

These are structural reasoning operators for Architecture D. They are not paper
exemplars, do not carry pattern IDs, and cannot accumulate evidence status.

## 1. Stage-boundary operator

Build every stage boundary as a complete causal handoff:

`current actor + current action → transition condition or threshold → observable pre-next-stage marker → next actor accepts or begins the next state → failed-transition branch`

The marker must be evidence produced by the current stage and observable before
the next stage's focal action. Do not use the first action of the next stage as
the marker. State why the marker licenses the inference that the process can
cross the boundary. Keep each proposition well formed by naming the actor,
action, condition, and next-state relation.

Close the stage chain at the terminal process state named in the task or
contribution claim. If the immediate next state is only an intermediate stage,
chain additional boundary crossings—each with its own transition condition and
marker—until the terminal state is reached. Never relabel a marker or an
intermediate state as the terminal outcome.

## 2. Feedback-transport operator

Build feedback as a transport chain rather than a direct arrow:

`feedback source → carrier or channel → translation or aggregation → decision-capable recipient → interpretation criterion → reinforce, redirect, or terminate → observable trace`

Distinguish the actor who generates feedback, the carrier who transmits or
translates it, and the recipient with authority to alter the process. Specify
what information survives translation, how the recipient interprets it, which
branch follows, and what observation would reveal that branch.

## QC

- Does the transition marker precede the next stage's focal action?
- Is the marker evidence of boundary crossing rather than a renamed stage outcome?
- Does the stage chain close at the terminal process state named in the task, rather than stopping at an intermediate next state?
- When the task requires an open empirical agenda, is it stated as a question whose rival answers map to different branches or interpretations of the same trace, rather than as a generic call for more research?
- Are source, carrier/channel, translator, and decision-capable recipient distinct where the setting requires them to be?
- Does every feedback branch produce a discriminating observable trace?
- Does each proposition identify actor, action, condition, and next-state relation?
