## Abstraction ladder: reasoning precision without model overfit

Use four levels. Fine-grained classification should improve comparison and conditional generation, not freeze a Top-journal paper's model form.

| Level | Preserve | Permitted effect | Overfitting boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L0 stable reasoning kernel** | Steelman rival accounts, establish their shared theoretical object/family, apply the route-specific formal lock, locate the incompatibility, state the resolution operator, derive a discriminating prediction, and preserve what remains valid in prior theory | May constrain all Incommensurability Theory sections after cross-paper validation | Do not require identical low-level X/Y before route selection or prescribe variable count, hypothesis count, mediation, moderation, or nonlinear form |
| **L1 resolution family** | R1 X differentiation, R2 Y disaggregation, R3 opposing mechanisms, or R4 contextual contingency | Select required reasoning tasks and candidate architectures | A route does not uniquely determine A–G variant or H numbering |
| **L2 hypothesis architecture** | Paired type predictions, outcome-specific branches, competing hypotheses, dominance schedules, conditional signs, and similar testable organizations | Store as an optional, evidence-tagged architecture | Promote only when the theoretical operation—not merely the model specification—recurs |
| **L3 paper/model signature** | Exact constructs, named theories, mediators, moderators, number/order of hypotheses, equations, labels, measures, and specific curve form | Keep as exemplar evidence only | Never infer a universal architecture from one paper's estimable model |

When route confidence is low, retain L0, report the two most plausible L1 routes, and postpone L2 selection. Permit `unclassified_residual`; do not add variables to make the paper fit R1–R4.

### Stable reasoning kernel

Before selecting A–G, recover:

1. **Conversation-level common object**: which umbrella construct, defensible higher-order family, or theoretical question makes the rival claims commensurable?
2. **Strong rival accounts**: what is the complete premise→mechanism→prediction chain for each side?
3. **Exact incompatibility**: which premise, mechanism, or prediction cannot jointly hold?
4. **Resolution operator**: partition X, partition Y, combine mechanisms, condition on W, or another operation.
5. **Combination/adjudication rule**: how does the operator generate a prediction that differs from the rivals?
6. **Null preservation**: under what subset or condition does each prior account remain valid?
7. **Testable closure**: what observation would distinguish the resolution from the strongest alternative?

### Two-stage comparability gate

1. **Conversation-level authenticity**: accept different lower-order X/Y indicators when they are explicitly mapped to one shared theoretical object or defensible higher-order family. Do not accept a shared topic label or an omnibus “firm outcomes” category without a theoretical mapping.
2. **R1/R2 member discipline**: the differentiated X₁/X₂ or Y₁/Y₂ need not be identical—that is the resolution. Define the umbrella construct/family and its members ex ante, and keep the unaffected comparison frame stable.
3. **R3/R4 formal lock**: before deriving hypotheses, freeze the concrete X, concrete Y, unit/level, time horizon, scope, and estimand. The rival predictions may differ only in mechanism/combination rule (R3) or W state (R4).

If R3/R4 requires switching outcomes, levels, or horizons to obtain opposite signs, reclassify toward R1/R2 or mark the conflict as apparent. Do not use conversation-level family membership as permission for construct switching inside a formal hypothesis pair.

## Route-to-architecture map

| Route | Candidate architecture | Optional supporting architecture | Minimum hypothesis logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R1 X-side differentiation** | A construct differentiation when X types redefine the construct; otherwise C hypothesis tree | B for type-specific mechanisms; F/G when rival predictions must be adjudicated | Define X₁/X₂, establish why the distinction matters, derive a separate Xᵢ→Y prediction for each type |
| **R2 Y-side disaggregation** | A when redefining the outcome family; otherwise C or B | F for paired rival predictions; G when outcomes represent sustained opposed consequences | Define Y₁/Y₂ at the same conceptual level, identify the mechanism linking X to each, and state outcome-specific predictions |
| **R3 opposing mechanisms** | G dialectical opposition or B2 dual-track mechanism | F when the design adjudicates competing theories; D when dominance changes through theorized stages; curvilinear subprotocol only when a dominance schedule is justified | Derive mechanism A and B symmetrically, specify how they combine, then predict the net pattern |
| **R4 contextual contingency** | E moderation | C for several theory-linked conditions; F when conditional predictions preserve a horse race | Show exactly how W changes exposure, capacity, interpretation, or mechanism dominance; derive directly distinguishable predictions for both W states |

The architectures are candidates, not automatic outputs. Do not let the coarse Gap × Makadok table override the conflict diagnosis, and do not let R1–R4 override the actual reasoning task or empirical design. Makadok levers describe the contribution; R1–R4 locate the conflict.

### Local evidence calibration (2026-08-04)

- **R1** has strict-core evidence from Vidal and Mitchell (2015).
- **R2** has secondary evidence from Pontikes (2012), Vidal and Mitchell (2015), and Bendig, Hensellek and Schulte (2024), subordinate to their respective R4, R1, and R3 primary resolutions.
- **R3** has strict-core evidence from Zhou et al. (2017), Bendig, Hensellek and Schulte (2024), and Anderson and Reeb (2004). Bendig et al. validate the move from a defensible higher-order outcome family to a fixed X–Y relation; Anderson and Reeb validate a relative-power X whose benefit and appropriation mechanisms switch dominance across the range.
- **R4** has strict-core evidence from Pontikes (2012), with Anderson and Reeb (2004) providing secondary evidence that an average ownership-form advantage can be decomposed by governance configuration.
- Keeves et al. (2017) and Park et al. (2025) fail the authenticity gate; Kundro and Rothbard (2023) is primarily Inadequacy × Boundary. Do not count these three as strict route validation. Vidal and Mitchell (2015) passes the gate as an R1-primary, R2-secondary exemplar; Bendig, Hensellek and Schulte (2024) passes as R3-primary, R2-secondary; Anderson and Reeb (2004) passes as R3-primary, R4-secondary.

Treat each evidenced route as EMERGING rather than ROBUST until independent full-text exemplars validate the same resolution operator.

## Derivation requirements

### R1: X-side differentiation

1. Define the umbrella X and the conceptual basis for X₁/X₂.
2. Establish discriminant validity: the types are not merely two measures or high/low bins.
3. Give each type a distinct mechanism or premise.
4. Choose the most economical testable form: paired predictions, one direct differential hypothesis, or a typological proposition. Do not force `H1a/H1b` when one contrast expresses the contribution.

### R2: Y-side disaggregation

1. Define the higher-order Y family and its theoretically distinct members.
2. Explain why prior aggregation obscures offsetting or functionally different consequences.
3. Re-run the why-chain for each Y rather than copying one mechanism and replacing the outcome label.
4. Use paired hypotheses only when the outcomes are both central and conceptually parallel; otherwise keep one primary outcome and treat the other as a boundary or ancillary consequence. Do not copy one why-chain and swap the Y label.

### R3: opposing mechanisms

1. Lock and state the concrete X, concrete Y, unit/level, time horizon, scope, and estimand so the conflict is not created by construct switching.
2. Build two distinct why-chains of comparable depth.
3. Specify the combination rule: stable dominance, changing dominance along X, threshold, temporal sequence, or context-dependent dominance.
4. Infer U/倒U only from a changing-dominance rule:
   - inverted U: the positive mechanism dominates at low-to-moderate X, then the negative mechanism grows faster and dominates at high X;
   - U: the negative mechanism dominates initially, then the positive mechanism grows faster and dominates at high X.
5. State the turning-point or slope-change prediction and use the appropriate nonlinear test. A quadratic coefficient alone is not the theory.
6. Do not require formal mediators unless the mechanisms are modeled as constructs and the design can test indirect effects; theoretical processes may remain B0 explanation.

### R4: contextual contingency

1. Lock the concrete X, concrete Y, unit/level, time horizon, scope, and estimand; then define W before discussing the interaction.
2. Identify the exact mechanism parameter W changes.
3. Derive both sides: X→Y across theoretically meaningful W states; do not force binary high/low language when W is categorical, continuous, nested, or threshold-based.
4. Use an interaction or direct conditional contrast. Do not infer moderation from significant/non-significant subgroup results.
5. If both conditional effects retain the same sign and only differ in strength, revisit the gap label; it is normally Inadequacy × Boundary.
6. Treat effect-versus-null as Incommensurability only when the null follows deductively from a rival account and the conditional difference is tested directly. Significant in one subgroup versus nonsignificant in another is insufficient.
7. Do not require a separate unconditional main-effect hypothesis. If no stable average effect follows, make the conditional prediction primary.

## Combination rules

- Prefer one primary route and at most one secondary route.
- Use **R3 + R4** when opposing mechanisms explain why a moderator reverses the net effect.
- Use **R1 + R2** only when the paper can support a theoretically sparse X-type × Y-type matrix; avoid enumerating every cell.
- Use **R2 + R4** when outcome differences are primary and context explains only one branch.
- Do not combine routes merely to increase model complexity.

## Anti-overfitting tests

1. **Construct-substitution test**: does the reasoning architecture remain coherent after replacing the source paper's constructs with another plausible R1–R4 case?
2. **Hypothesis-count invariance**: does the pattern survive if H numbering, branch count, or paragraph count changes? If not, it is L3.
3. **Model-form necessity**: is mediation, moderation, competition, threshold, or U/倒U required by the theory, or merely the source paper's estimable specification?
4. **Simpler-architecture test**: can one differential or conditional hypothesis express the same contribution more cleanly? If yes, do not promote the more complex form.
5. **Discriminating-prediction test**: does the extracted architecture yield an observation that separates it from both prior accounts?
6. **Measurement firewall**: would the route disappear if proxies or coding choices changed while the theoretical constructs remained? If yes, do not promote it as theory architecture.

## Evolution hook

For each future full-text theory exemplar, record L0–L3, the observed R1–R4 route, candidate and selected architecture, combination rule, and adjudicating prediction. Log papers that require another conflict location or contradict a route boundary as design feedback; do not force them into the matrix or promote one paper's model signature to a universal rule.
